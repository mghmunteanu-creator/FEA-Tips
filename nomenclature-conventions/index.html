01

Notation

Scalars are written with italic letters. Vectors and matrices are written with bold italic letters.

Abstract vector and matrix symbols are written without brackets. When their components are displayed explicitly, a row vector uses parentheses, a column vector uses braces, and a matrix uses square brackets.

Π, U, V, E, A, L, u, v\Pi,\ U,\ V,\ E,\ A,\ L,\ u,\ vΠ, U, V, E, A, L, u, v
Scalars
K, kel, fel, u, uel\boldsymbol{K},\ \boldsymbol{k}_{el},\ \boldsymbol{f}_{el},\ \boldsymbol{u},\ \boldsymbol{u}_{el}K, kel​, fel​, u, uel​
Vectors and matrices. Their dimensions follow from context: for example, u\boldsymbol{u}u and fel\boldsymbol{f}_{el}fel​ are column vectors, whereasK\boldsymbol{K}K and kel\boldsymbol{k}_{el}kel​ are matrices.
K‾, k‾el, f‾el, u‾\overline{\boldsymbol{K}},\ \overline{\boldsymbol{k}}_{el},\ \overline{\boldsymbol{f}}_{el},\ \overline{\boldsymbol{u}}K, kel​, f​el​, u
A bar denotes a vector or tensor expressed in the local reference frame. Symbols without a bar refer to the global reference frame.

Sign convention for beams

Axial force and normal stress are positive in tension and negative in compression.

Section 4 retains the coordinate convention used in its finite-difference derivation: the global xxx axis points to the right and its yyy axis points downward. This chapter-specific choice is stated in its figure and MATLAB program.

Virtual work and energy principles

For a linear elastic structure with a symmetric stiffness matrix, the total potential energy is

Π=12∑eluelTkeluel−uTF=12uTKu−uTF\Pi=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{el}\boldsymbol{u}_{el}^{T}\boldsymbol{k}_{el}\boldsymbol{u}_{el}-\boldsymbol{u}^{T}\boldsymbol{F}=\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{u}^{T}\boldsymbol{K}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}^{T}\boldsymbol{F}Π=21​el∑​uelT​kel​uel​−uTF=21​uTKu−uTF

Minimum total potential energy

Ψ=∂Π∂u=Ku−F=0\boldsymbol{\Psi}=\frac{\partial\Pi}{\partial\boldsymbol{u}}=\boldsymbol{K}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{F}=\boldsymbol{0}Ψ=∂u∂Π​=Ku−F=0

Principle of virtual work

For every geometrically admissible virtual displacementδu\delta\boldsymbol{u}δu,

δΠ=δuT(Ku−F)=0\delta\Pi=\delta\boldsymbol{u}^{T}\left(\boldsymbol{K}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{F}\right)=0δΠ=δuT(Ku−F)=0

Both principles lead to the same equilibrium equations:

Ku=F\boldsymbol{K}\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{F}Ku=F

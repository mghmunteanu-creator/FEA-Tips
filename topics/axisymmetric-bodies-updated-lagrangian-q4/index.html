01

Introduction

This section develops a direct and consistent Updated Lagrangian (UL) formulation for the four-node isoparametric quadrilateral element used in axisymmetric analysis.

The corresponding Total Lagrangian (TL) formulation was presented in Section 28.3. In that formulation, the initial geometry is the reference configuration and the Green–Lagrange strain tensor is employed. Here, the internal forces and their consistent linearization are evaluated in the current trial configuration, using Cauchy stresses and total Euler–Almansi strains.

The distinction between these strain measures was explained in Section 28.2. The present development follows the direct linearization approach introduced in Section 15.5 and extended to three-dimensional elements in Section 27.2.

The tangent stiffness matrix is obtained directly by differentiating the internal force vector. The formulation uses ordinary matrices, vectors and their derivatives; no fourth-order tensors are required. General background on nonlinear finite element kinematics and consistent linearization is given in [1] and [2].

Axisymmetric analysis introduces two additional features compared with conventional two-dimensional plane problems: the circumferential strain and stress components, and the radial coordinate in the integration measure. Both must be included in the consistent linearization. The objective is an explicit element tangent stiffness matrix suitable for direct implementation in MATLAB.

Readers interested in a less demanding numerical implementation can consult Section 28.4b, which presents an approximate incremental approach based on applying the Total Lagrangian formulation at each load step, using the configuration reached at the end of the preceding step as the new reference configuration.

Axisymmetric kinematics

2.1. Initial and current configurations

The yyy-axis is the axis of symmetry, rrr is the radial coordinate and θ\thetaθ is the circumferential coordinate. We use R,YR,YR,Y for the initial coordinates and r,yr,yr,y for the current coordinates. The displacement field is

ur=r−R,uy=y−Yu_r=r-R,\qquad u_y=y-Yur​=r−R,uy​=y−Y

For axisymmetric deformation without circumferential displacement,

uθ=0,∂(⋅)∂θ=0u_\theta=0,\qquad\frac{\partial(\cdot)}{\partial\theta}=0uθ​=0,∂θ∂(⋅)​=0

The meridional deformation gradient is

Fm=[∂r∂R∂r∂Y∂y∂R∂y∂Y]=[1+∂ur∂R∂ur∂Y∂uy∂R1+∂uy∂Y]\boldsymbol F_m=\begin{bmatrix}\dfrac{\partial r}{\partial R}&\dfrac{\partial r}{\partial Y}\\[4pt]\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial R}&\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial Y}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1+\dfrac{\partial u_r}{\partial R}&\dfrac{\partial u_r}{\partial Y}\\[4pt]\dfrac{\partial u_y}{\partial R}&1+\dfrac{\partial u_y}{\partial Y}\end{bmatrix}Fm​=​∂R∂r​∂R∂y​​∂Y∂r​∂Y∂y​​​=​1+∂R∂ur​​∂R∂uy​​​∂Y∂ur​​1+∂Y∂uy​​​​

The circumferential stretch is

λθ=rR\lambda_\theta=\frac rRλθ​=Rr​

Thus, in the orthonormal cylindrical basis, the complete deformation gradient has the block-diagonal form

F=[FrrFry0FyrFyy000λθ]\boldsymbol F=\begin{bmatrix}F_{rr}&F_{ry}&0\\F_{yr}&F_{yy}&0\\0&0&\lambda_\theta\end{bmatrix}F=​Frr​Fyr​0​Fry​Fyy​0​00λθ​​​

The deformation is axisymmetric, but this is not a plane-stress or plane-strain problem: circumferential deformation must be included.

2.2. Total Euler–Almansi strains

The Euler–Almansi strain tensor is defined by

e=12(I−F−TF−1)\boldsymbol e=\frac12\left(\boldsymbol I-\boldsymbol F^{-T}\boldsymbol F^{-1}\right)e=21​(I−F−TF−1)

For the meridional components, introduce

Q=(FmFmT)−1\boldsymbol Q=(\boldsymbol F_m\boldsymbol F_m^T)^{-1}Q=(Fm​FmT​)−1

Then

em=12(I−Q)=[ereryeryey]\boldsymbol e_m=\frac12(\boldsymbol I-\boldsymbol Q)=\begin{bmatrix}e_r&e_{ry}\\e_{ry}&e_y\end{bmatrix}em​=21​(I−Q)=[er​ery​​ery​ey​​]

Consequently,

er=12(1−Q11),ey=12(1−Q22),γry=2ery=−Q12e_r=\frac12(1-Q_{11}),\qquad e_y=\frac12(1-Q_{22}),\qquad\gamma_{ry}=2e_{ry}=-Q_{12}er​=21​(1−Q11​),ey​=21​(1−Q22​),γry​=2ery​=−Q12​

The circumferential strain follows directly from the stretch r/Rr/Rr/R:

eθ=12(1−R2r2)e_\theta=\frac12\left(1-\frac{R^2}{r^2}\right)eθ​=21​(1−r2R2​)

The engineering strain vector is

ε={ereyeθγry}T\boldsymbol\varepsilon=\begin{Bmatrix}e_r&e_y&e_\theta&\gamma_{ry}\end{Bmatrix}^Tε={er​​ey​​eθ​​γry​​}T

Green–Lagrange strains refer to the initial configuration, whereas Euler–Almansi strains refer to the current configuration. They describe the same deformation using different reference configurations. The distinction is particularly clear for the circumferential components:

Eθ=12[(rR)2−1],eθ=12[1−(Rr)2]E_\theta=\frac12\left[\left(\frac rR\right)^2-1\right],\qquad e_\theta=\frac12\left[1-\left(\frac Rr\right)^2\right]Eθ​=21​[(Rr​)2−1],eθ​=21​[1−(rR​)2]

Isoparametric Q4 element

3.1. Interpolation functions

We use the four-node quadrilateral element of Section 28.3. With natural coordinates s,ts,ts,t, its interpolation functions are

N1=14(1+s)(1+t)N2=14(1−s)(1+t)N3=14(1−s)(1−t)N4=14(1+s)(1−t)\begin{aligned}N_1&=\tfrac14(1+s)(1+t)&N_2&=\tfrac14(1-s)(1+t)\\N_3&=\tfrac14(1-s)(1-t)&N_4&=\tfrac14(1+s)(1-t)\end{aligned}N1​N3​​=41​(1+s)(1+t)=41​(1−s)(1−t)​N2​N4​​=41​(1−s)(1+t)=41​(1+s)(1−t)​

The natural-coordinate node numbering is preserved in the connectivity of the physical element. The current and initial coordinates are interpolated as

r(s,t)=∑i=14Ni(s,t)ri,y(s,t)=∑i=14Ni(s,t)yir(s,t)=\sum_{i=1}^4N_i(s,t)r_i,\qquad y(s,t)=\sum_{i=1}^4N_i(s,t)y_ir(s,t)=i=1∑4​Ni​(s,t)ri​,y(s,t)=i=1∑4​Ni​(s,t)yi​

Define the natural-derivative matrix

H=[N1,sN2,sN3,sN4,sN1,tN2,tN3,tN4,t]\boldsymbol H=\begin{bmatrix}N_{1,s}&N_{2,s}&N_{3,s}&N_{4,s}\\N_{1,t}&N_{2,t}&N_{3,t}&N_{4,t}\end{bmatrix}H=[N1,s​N1,t​​N2,s​N2,t​​N3,s​N3,t​​N4,s​N4,t​​]

The Jacobian matrices are

J0=H[R1Y1R2Y2R3Y3R4Y4],Jt=H[r1y1r2y2r3y3r4y4]\boldsymbol J_0=\boldsymbol H\begin{bmatrix}R_1&Y_1\\R_2&Y_2\\R_3&Y_3\\R_4&Y_4\end{bmatrix},\qquad\boldsymbol J_t=\boldsymbol H\begin{bmatrix}r_1&y_1\\r_2&y_2\\r_3&y_3\\r_4&y_4\end{bmatrix}J0​=H​R1​R2​R3​R4​​Y1​Y2​Y3​Y4​​​,Jt​=H​r1​r2​r3​r4​​y1​y2​y3​y4​​​

The meridional deformation gradient is evaluated directly from these matrices:

Fm=(J0−1Jt)T\boldsymbol F_m=(\boldsymbol J_0^{-1}\boldsymbol J_t)^TFm​=(J0−1​Jt​)T

This expression is convenient for MATLAB implementation. According to the FEA Tips element-orientation convention, the Jacobian determinants must be positive for the valid element configurations considered.

3.2. Spatial derivatives

The interpolation derivatives with respect to the current coordinates are

b=Jt−1H=[br1br2br3br4by1by2by3by4]\boldsymbol b=\boldsymbol J_t^{-1}\boldsymbol H=\begin{bmatrix}b_{r1}&b_{r2}&b_{r3}&b_{r4}\\b_{y1}&b_{y2}&b_{y3}&b_{y4}\end{bmatrix}b=Jt−1​H=[br1​by1​​br2​by2​​br3​by3​​br4​by4​​]

Thus,

bri=∂Ni∂r,byi=∂Ni∂yb_{ri}=\frac{\partial N_i}{\partial r},\qquad b_{yi}=\frac{\partial N_i}{\partial y}bri​=∂r∂Ni​​,byi​=∂y∂Ni​​

These spatial derivatives are evaluated in the current trial configuration.

3.3. Axisymmetric strain–displacement matrix

Let the virtual nodal displacement vector of one element be

δuel={δur1δuy1⋯δur4δuy4}T\delta\boldsymbol u_{el}=\begin{Bmatrix}\delta u_{r1}&\delta u_{y1}&\cdots&\delta u_{r4}&\delta u_{y4}\end{Bmatrix}^Tδuel​={δur1​​δuy1​​⋯​δur4​​δuy4​​}T

The infinitesimal spatial strain variation associated with this virtual displacement is

δεsp=Bδuel\delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{sp}=\boldsymbol B\delta\boldsymbol u_{el}δεsp​=Bδuel​

The axisymmetric matrix B\boldsymbol BB has four rows and eight columns. Its 4×24\times24×2 block for node iii is

Bi=[bri00byiNi/r0byibri],B=[B1B2B3B4]\boldsymbol B_i=\begin{bmatrix}b_{ri}&0\\0&b_{yi}\\N_i/r&0\\b_{yi}&b_{ri}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\boldsymbol B=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol B_1&\boldsymbol B_2&\boldsymbol B_3&\boldsymbol B_4\end{bmatrix}Bi​=​bri​0Ni​/rbyi​​0byi​0bri​​​,B=[B1​​B2​​B3​​B4​​]

The third row is the circumferential contribution. It does not appear in ordinary plane-stress or plane-strain elements and is an essential difference introduced by axisymmetry.

04

Constitutive relationship

The material is assumed to be isotropic and linearly elastic. The stress vector is

σ={σrσyσθτry}T\boldsymbol\sigma=\begin{Bmatrix}\sigma_r&\sigma_y&\sigma_\theta&\tau_{ry}\end{Bmatrix}^Tσ={σr​​σy​​σθ​​τry​​}T

We adopt the relationship

σ=Dε\boldsymbol\sigma=\boldsymbol D\boldsymbol\varepsilonσ=Dε

Here, σ\boldsymbol\sigmaσ contains Cauchy stress components and ε\boldsymbol\varepsilonε contains total Euler–Almansi engineering strain components. Following the convention of Section 28.1, the constitutive matrix is

D=E(1+ν)(1−2ν)[1−ννν0ν1−νν0νν1−ν00001−2ν2]\boldsymbol D=\frac{E}{(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)}\begin{bmatrix}1-\nu&\nu&\nu&0\\\nu&1-\nu&\nu&0\\\nu&\nu&1-\nu&0\\0&0&0&\dfrac{1-2\nu}{2}\end{bmatrix}D=(1+ν)(1−2ν)E​​1−ννν0​ν1−νν0​νν1−ν0​00021−2ν​​​

Although this matrix is constant, the structural response is nonlinear because the Euler–Almansi strains, element geometry and internal forces depend nonlinearly on the displacements. The engineering shear convention is retained.

This is the same type of total-strain relationship used in Section 15.5 and Section 27.2. It is distinct from the TL relationship

S=DE\boldsymbol S=\boldsymbol D\boldsymbol ES=DE

used in Section 28.3, where S\boldsymbol SS and E\boldsymbol EE denote the second Piola–Kirchhoff stress and Green–Lagrange strain in engineering-vector form. At finite deformation, these are different constitutive assumptions unless the stresses and tangent moduli are transformed consistently between configurations. This distinction matters when comparing TL and UL results.

Internal force vector for a one-radian sector

5.1. Axisymmetric volume integration

The axisymmetric volume element is

dV=r dr dy dθdV=r\,dr\,dy\,d\thetadV=rdrdydθ

The calculations use a circumferential sector of one radian, so

∫dθ=1,dV=r dA\int d\theta=1,\qquad dV=r\,dA∫dθ=1,dV=rdA

where dAdAdA is the differential area in the current meridional plane. In natural coordinates,

dV=r(s,t)det⁡ ⁣(Jt(s,t)) ds dtdV=r(s,t)\det\!\big(\boldsymbol J_t(s,t)\big)\,ds\,dtdV=r(s,t)det(Jt​(s,t))dsdt

The same one-radian convention applies to external forces and pressure resultants.

5.2. Internal forces

In the current trial configuration, the element internal force vector is

fintel=∫ΩtelBTσ r dA\boldsymbol f_{int}^{el}=\int_{\Omega_t^{el}}\boldsymbol B^T\boldsymbol\sigma\,r\,dAfintel​=∫Ωtel​​BTσrdA

or, in natural coordinates,

fintel=∫−11∫−11rBTσdet⁡(Jt) ds dt\boldsymbol f_{int}^{el}=\int_{-1}^1\int_{-1}^1r\boldsymbol B^T\boldsymbol\sigma\det(\boldsymbol J_t)\,ds\,dtfintel​=∫−11​∫−11​rBTσdet(Jt​)dsdt

The Q4 element uses the 2×22\times22×2 Gauss rule,

s,t=±13s,t=\pm\frac1{\sqrt3}s,t=±3​1​

with unit weights. Hence,

fintel≈∑g=14rgBgTσgdet⁡(Jt,g)\boldsymbol f_{int}^{el}\approx\sum_{g=1}^4r_g\boldsymbol B_g^T\boldsymbol\sigma_g\det(\boldsymbol J_{t,g})fintel​≈g=1∑4​rg​BgT​σg​det(Jt,g​)

Both the matrices and integration factors are evaluated in the current trial configuration. The nonlinear equilibrium equations are

R(u)=fint(u)−fext=0\boldsymbol R(\boldsymbol u)=\boldsymbol f_{int}(\boldsymbol u)-\boldsymbol f_{ext}=\boldsymbol0R(u)=fint​(u)−fext​=0

The tangent stiffness matrix is obtained by differentiating this residual with respect to the nodal displacements.

06

Direct consistent linearization

As in Section 15.5, we linearize the internal force vector directly, including the variations of strains, interpolation derivatives and integration measure. For one element,

δfintel=kelδuel\delta\boldsymbol f_{int}^{el}=\boldsymbol k^{el}\delta\boldsymbol u_{el}δfintel​=kelδuel​

The tangent consists of material and geometric contributions:

kel=kmatel+kgeomel\boldsymbol k^{el}=\boldsymbol k_{mat}^{el}+\boldsymbol k_{geom}^{el}kel=kmatel​+kgeomel​

6.1. Variation of the total Euler–Almansi strains

Recall that

em=12(I−Q),Q=(FmFmT)−1\boldsymbol e_m=\frac12(\boldsymbol I-\boldsymbol Q),\qquad\boldsymbol Q=(\boldsymbol F_m\boldsymbol F_m^T)^{-1}em​=21​(I−Q),Q=(Fm​FmT​)−1

For an infinitesimal variation of the current configuration, define the spatial displacement-gradient variation

δL=∂(δu)∂x,x={ry}\delta\boldsymbol L=\frac{\partial(\delta\boldsymbol u)}{\partial\boldsymbol x},\qquad\boldsymbol x=\begin{Bmatrix}r\\y\end{Bmatrix}δL=∂x∂(δu)​,x={ry​}

The deformation-gradient variation satisfies

δFm=δL Fm\delta\boldsymbol F_m=\delta\boldsymbol L\,\boldsymbol F_mδFm​=δLFm​

Consequently,

δQ=−(δL)TQ−Q δL\delta\boldsymbol Q=-(\delta\boldsymbol L)^T\boldsymbol Q-\boldsymbol Q\,\delta\boldsymbol LδQ=−(δL)TQ−QδL

and therefore

δem=12[(δL)TQ+Q δL]\delta\boldsymbol e_m=\frac12\left[(\delta\boldsymbol L)^T\boldsymbol Q+\boldsymbol Q\,\delta\boldsymbol L\right]δem​=21​[(δL)TQ+QδL]

This relationship is the basis for the consistent material tangent.

6.2. The matrix BQ\boldsymbol B_QBQ​

The variation of the total Euler–Almansi engineering strain vector is

δε=BQδuel\delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon=\boldsymbol B_Q\delta\boldsymbol u_{el}δε=BQ​δuel​

Unlike the ordinary spatial matrix B\boldsymbol BB, BQ\boldsymbol B_QBQ​ contains Q\boldsymbol QQ. Its meridional terms follow directly from the preceding variation. The circumferential contribution is derived separately. Since

eθ=12(1−R2r2)e_\theta=\frac12\left(1-\frac{R^2}{r^2}\right)eθ​=21​(1−r2R2​)

and RRR remains constant during the variation of the current configuration,

δeθ=R2r3δr\delta e_\theta=\frac{R^2}{r^3}\delta rδeθ​=r3R2​δr

With

δr=∑i=14Niδuri\delta r=\sum_{i=1}^4N_i\delta u_{ri}δr=i=1∑4​Ni​δuri​

the circumferential row for node iii is

(BQ,i)θ=[NiR2/r30]\left(\boldsymbol B_{Q,i}\right)_\theta=\begin{bmatrix}N_iR^2/r^3&0\end{bmatrix}(BQ,i​)θ​=[Ni​R2/r3​0​]

The complete 4×24\times24×2 block is

BQ,i=[Q11briQ12briQ12byiQ22byiNiR2/r30Q12bri+Q11byiQ22bri+Q12byi]\boldsymbol B_{Q,i}=\begin{bmatrix}Q_{11}b_{ri}&Q_{12}b_{ri}\\Q_{12}b_{yi}&Q_{22}b_{yi}\\N_iR^2/r^3&0\\Q_{12}b_{ri}+Q_{11}b_{yi}&Q_{22}b_{ri}+Q_{12}b_{yi}\end{bmatrix}BQ,i​=​Q11​bri​Q12​byi​Ni​R2/r3Q12​bri​+Q11​byi​​Q12​bri​Q22​byi​0Q22​bri​+Q12​byi​​​

and

BQ=[BQ,1BQ,2BQ,3BQ,4]\boldsymbol B_Q=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol B_{Q,1}&\boldsymbol B_{Q,2}&\boldsymbol B_{Q,3}&\boldsymbol B_{Q,4}\end{bmatrix}BQ​=[BQ,1​​BQ,2​​BQ,3​​BQ,4​​]

The first, second and fourth rows follow the same principle as the consistent plane formulation in Section 15.5. The third row is specific to axisymmetry. In particular,

(Bi)θ=[Ni/r0],(BQ,i)θ=[NiR2/r30]\left(\boldsymbol B_i\right)_\theta=\begin{bmatrix}N_i/r&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\left(\boldsymbol B_{Q,i}\right)_\theta=\begin{bmatrix}N_iR^2/r^3&0\end{bmatrix}(Bi​)θ​=[Ni​/r​0​],(BQ,i​)θ​=[Ni​R2/r3​0​]

Replacing the second expression by the first would give an inconsistent material tangent at finite deformation.

6.3. Material contribution to the tangent stiffness

Variation of the constitutive relationship gives

δσ=Dδε=DBQδuel\delta\boldsymbol\sigma=\boldsymbol D\delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon=\boldsymbol D\boldsymbol B_Q\delta\boldsymbol u_{el}δσ=Dδε=DBQ​δuel​

Its contribution to the internal force variation is

δfmatel=[∫ΩtelrBTDBQ dA]δuel\delta\boldsymbol f_{mat}^{el}=\left[\int_{\Omega_t^{el}}r\boldsymbol B^T\boldsymbol D\boldsymbol B_Q\,dA\right]\delta\boldsymbol u_{el}δfmatel​=[∫Ωtel​​rBTDBQ​dA]δuel​

Therefore,

kmatel=∫ΩtelrBTDBQ dA\boldsymbol k_{mat}^{el}=\int_{\Omega_t^{el}}r\boldsymbol B^T\boldsymbol D\boldsymbol B_Q\,dAkmatel​=∫Ωtel​​rBTDBQ​dA

or, in natural coordinates,

kmatel=∫−11∫−11rBTDBQdet⁡(Jt) ds dt\boldsymbol k_{mat}^{el}=\int_{-1}^1\int_{-1}^1r\boldsymbol B^T\boldsymbol D\boldsymbol B_Q\det(\boldsymbol J_t)\,ds\,dtkmatel​=∫−11​∫−11​rBTDBQ​det(Jt​)dsdt

This is analogous to Section 15.5, with four axisymmetric strain components and the additional radial integration factor.

07

Consistent geometric tangent stiffness

The spatial derivatives, radial coordinate and integration measure all depend on the current configuration and contribute to the geometric tangent.

7.1. Variation of the spatial derivatives

Define the spatial gradient of NiN_iNi​:

gi={bribyi}\boldsymbol g_i=\begin{Bmatrix}b_{ri}\\b_{yi}\end{Bmatrix}gi​={bri​byi​​}

Its variation is

δgi=−(δL)Tgi\delta\boldsymbol g_i=-(\delta\boldsymbol L)^T\boldsymbol g_iδgi​=−(δL)Tgi​

This is the same basic relationship used in the direct consistent linearization of Section 15.5.

7.2. Variation of the axisymmetric integration measure

For the meridional area element,

δ(dA)=tr⁡(δL) dA\delta(dA)=\operatorname{tr}(\delta\boldsymbol L)\,dAδ(dA)=tr(δL)dA

The axisymmetric volume measure is r dAr\,dArdA, whose variation is

δ(r dA)=r δ(dA)+δr dA\delta(r\,dA)=r\,\delta(dA)+\delta r\,dAδ(rdA)=rδ(dA)+δrdA

or

δ(r dA)=[tr⁡(δL)+δrr]r dA\delta(r\,dA)=\left[\operatorname{tr}(\delta\boldsymbol L)+\frac{\delta r}{r}\right]r\,dAδ(rdA)=[tr(δL)+rδr​]rdA

The second term is specific to axisymmetric deformation: it represents the variation of the circumferential dimension.

7.3. Variation of the circumferential interpolation term

The circumferential row of B\boldsymbol BB contains Ni/rN_i/rNi​/r. Since NiN_iNi​ is defined in natural coordinates, its value does not change during the variation. Consequently,

δ(Nir)=−Nir2δr\delta\left(\frac{N_i}{r}\right)=-\frac{N_i}{r^2}\delta rδ(rNi​​)=−r2Ni​​δr

When differentiating the internal force, this term is combined with the variation of the radial integration factor. The two terms involving the direct variation of rrr in the circumferential force contribution cancel. The remaining terms contribute to the geometric stiffness.

7.4. Explicit geometric stiffness blocks

Introduce the meridional Cauchy stress matrix and the unit radial vector:

σm=[σrτryτryσy],er={10}\boldsymbol\sigma_m=\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_r&\tau_{ry}\\\tau_{ry}&\sigma_y\end{bmatrix},\qquad\boldsymbol e_r=\begin{Bmatrix}1\\0\end{Bmatrix}σm​=[σr​τry​​τry​σy​​],er​={10​}

The contribution associated with nodes iii and jjj is a 2×22\times22×2 block. Collecting the variations of spatial derivatives, radial coordinate and integration measure gives

kg,ij∗=(σmgi)gjT−(σmgj)giT+Njr(σmgi)erT+NiσθrergjT\begin{aligned}\boldsymbol k_{g,ij}^{*}={}&(\boldsymbol\sigma_m\boldsymbol g_i)\boldsymbol g_j^T-(\boldsymbol\sigma_m\boldsymbol g_j)\boldsymbol g_i^T\\&+\frac{N_j}{r}(\boldsymbol\sigma_m\boldsymbol g_i)\boldsymbol e_r^T+\frac{N_i\sigma_\theta}{r}\boldsymbol e_r\boldsymbol g_j^T\end{aligned}kg,ij∗​=​(σm​gi​)gjT​−(σm​gj​)giT​+rNj​​(σm​gi​)erT​+rNi​σθ​​er​gjT​​

The first two terms are analogous to the plane UL geometric contribution. The last two arise from axisymmetry; the fourth contains the circumferential Cauchy stress σθ\sigma_\thetaσθ​. The geometric matrix is assembled from these blocks:

kg∗=[kg,11∗⋯kg,14∗⋮⋱⋮kg,41∗⋯kg,44∗]\boldsymbol k_g^*=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol k_{g,11}^*&\cdots&\boldsymbol k_{g,14}^*\\\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\\boldsymbol k_{g,41}^*&\cdots&\boldsymbol k_{g,44}^*\end{bmatrix}kg∗​=​kg,11∗​⋮kg,41∗​​⋯⋱⋯​kg,14∗​⋮kg,44∗​​​

Thus,

kgeomel=∫Ωtelrkg∗ dA\boldsymbol k_{geom}^{el}=\int_{\Omega_t^{el}}r\boldsymbol k_g^*\,dAkgeomel​=∫Ωtel​​rkg∗​dA

or

kgeomel=∫−11∫−11rkg∗det⁡(Jt) ds dt\boldsymbol k_{geom}^{el}=\int_{-1}^1\int_{-1}^1r\boldsymbol k_g^*\det(\boldsymbol J_t)\,ds\,dtkgeomel​=∫−11​∫−11​rkg∗​det(Jt​)dsdt

For the geometric part, the Cauchy stresses are held fixed when differentiating the internal force expression. Their variation is already included in the material contribution. No additional geometric correction is needed when all the terms above are retained.

7.5. Final consistent element tangent matrix

Combining the two contributions gives

kel=∫−11∫−11r(BTDBQ+kg∗)det⁡(Jt) ds dt\boldsymbol k^{el}=\int_{-1}^1\int_{-1}^1r\left(\boldsymbol B^T\boldsymbol D\boldsymbol B_Q+\boldsymbol k_g^*\right)\det(\boldsymbol J_t)\,ds\,dtkel=∫−11​∫−11​r(BTDBQ​+kg∗​)det(Jt​)dsdt

For the 2×22\times22×2 Gauss rule,

kel≈∑g=14rg(BgTDBQ,g+kg,g∗)det⁡(Jt,g)\boldsymbol k^{el}\approx\sum_{g=1}^4r_g\left(\boldsymbol B_g^T\boldsymbol D\boldsymbol B_{Q,g}+\boldsymbol k_{g,g}^*\right)\det(\boldsymbol J_{t,g})kel≈g=1∑4​rg​(BgT​DBQ,g​+kg,g∗​)det(Jt,g​)

This is the principal result: a direct and consistent tangent stiffness matrix for the axisymmetric Q4 element, expressed entirely in conventional matrices and vectors. No fourth-order constitutive tensors or additional stress-update algorithm is required for the adopted total-strain law.

The tangent is not artificially symmetrized. Although D\boldsymbol DD is symmetric, the complete direct tangent need not be symmetric for the constitutive relationship used here.

08

MATLAB implementation

The program follows the consistent UL implementations of Section 15.5 and Section 27.2.

gen.m

Generates the axisymmetric Q4 mesh, coordinates, connectivity, material properties, constraints and external nodal loads. The toroidal-container geometry of Section 28.3 is used for comparison.

main.m

Performs load stepping and Newton–Raphson iteration. Each current trial configuration is used to assemble the internal force vector and consistent tangent. For the global residual,

R=fint−fext,KTΔu=−R\boldsymbol R=\boldsymbol f_{int}-\boldsymbol f_{ext},\qquad\boldsymbol K_T\Delta\boldsymbol u=-\boldsymbol RR=fint​−fext​,KT​Δu=−R

The trial configuration is updated until both the displacement-correction and relative force-residual tolerances are met. The supplied program uses tol=1e-4, tolF=1e-8 and at most 50 iterations per step. Failure to converge stops the calculation with an error.   2. Calculates Fm\boldsymbol F_mFm​ and Q\boldsymbol QQ.

9.2. External loading

To avoid the additional complexity associated with follower pressure loads and their consistent external-load tangent, the pressure was represented by equivalent nodal forces determined from the initial geometry. Their magnitudes and directions were kept constant throughout the deformation process. The one-radian convention and the nodal loading rule p dL rp\,dL\,rpdLr, including the end nodes, are retained.

The same simplification was adopted in the ANSYS analysis: nodal forces corresponding to the initial geometry were prescribed instead of a continuously updated pressure load. Thus, the MATLAB and ANSYS analyses were performed under the same loading conditions, allowing a meaningful comparison of their numerical results.

Since the prescribed external force vector does not depend on the current displacements, the consistent Newton–Raphson tangent is obtained by differentiating the internal force vector alone.

10

Conclusions

The direct and consistent UL formulation for the axisymmetric Q4 element extends Section 15.5 by including circumferential deformation and stress and the radial integration factor.

Total Euler–Almansi strains are calculated from the deformation gradient between the initial and current configurations. Internal forces use Cauchy stresses in the current trial configuration; their direct linearization gives material and geometric tangent contributions.

The additional axisymmetric terms come from the circumferential strain variation, the derivative of the circumferential interpolation term, the radial integration-factor variation, and the circumferential stress contribution to geometric stiffness. All are necessary for consistency.

The formulation uses standard matrices and vectors and is implemented in a compact MATLAB program. The toroidal-container example demonstrates its application, rapid Newton–Raphson convergence and satisfactory agreement with the TL and ANSYS results.

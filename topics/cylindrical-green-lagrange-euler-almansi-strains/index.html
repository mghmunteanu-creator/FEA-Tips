As discussed in Chapter 13, the Green–Lagrange strain tensor refers to the initial configuration, whereas the Euler–Almansi strain tensor refers to the current configuration. Here, both measures are expressed in cylindrical coordinates, including the particular case of axisymmetric deformation.

01

Cylindrical coordinates and deformation gradient

The radial coordinate is rrr, the axis of symmetry is yyy, and the circumferential coordinate is θ\thetaθ.

05

Euler–Almansi strain tensor

The Euler–Almansi strain tensor refers to the current configuration. Using the same deformation gradient and displacement variables, it is defined by [3]:

e=12(I−F−TF−1)\boldsymbol e=\frac12\left(\boldsymbol I-\boldsymbol F^{-T}\boldsymbol F^{-1}\right)e=21​(I−F−TF−1)

It is related to the Green–Lagrange tensor by:

e=F−TEF−1\boldsymbol e=\boldsymbol F^{-T}\boldsymbol E\boldsymbol F^{-1}e=F−TEF−1

For the general cylindrical formulation, the matrix F\boldsymbol FF given above is used directly. In the coordinate order (r,y,θ)(r,y,\theta)(r,y,θ), the engineering components are arranged as:

e=[εrγry/2γθr/2γry/2εyγyθ/2γθr/2γyθ/2εθ]=12[I−(FFT)−1]\boldsymbol e=\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon_r&\gamma_{ry}/2&\gamma_{\theta r}/2\\\gamma_{ry}/2&\varepsilon_y&\gamma_{y\theta}/2\\\gamma_{\theta r}/2&\gamma_{y\theta}/2&\varepsilon_\theta\end{bmatrix}=\frac12\left[\boldsymbol I-(\boldsymbol F\boldsymbol F^T)^{-1}\right]e=​εr​γry​/2γθr​/2​γry​/2εy​γyθ​/2​γθr​/2γyθ​/2εθ​​​=21​[I−(FFT)−1]

07

Initial and current configurations

Both strain measures describe the same deformation, but they refer to different configurations. The circumferential strains provide a simple illustration:

Green–Lagrange:

εθ=12[(r+ur)2−1]\varepsilon_\theta=\frac12\left[\left(\frac{r+u}{r}\right)^2-1\right]εθ​=21​[(rr+u​)2−1]

Euler–Almansi:

εθ=12[1−(rr+u)2]\varepsilon_\theta=\frac12\left[1-\left(\frac{r}{r+u}\right)^2\right]εθ​=21​[1−(r+ur​)2]

Green–Lagrange uses the initial radius rrr as reference, whereas Euler–Almansi refers to the current radius r+ur+ur+u.

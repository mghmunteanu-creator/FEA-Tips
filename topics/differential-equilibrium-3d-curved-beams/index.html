A 3D slender curved beam may be regarded as generated by a cross-section moving along a three-dimensional curved centroidal axis while remaining normal to it. The centroid of each cross-section lies on this curve, and the dimensions of the cross-section are small compared with the other dimensions of the beam.

At every point of the centroidal axis, the rotation matrix R\boldsymbol{R}R is known as a function of the curvilinear coordinate sss. As in Section 22.1, R\boldsymbol{R}R is the passive transformation matrix from the global reference frame to the local reference frame:

vˉ=Rv. \bar{\boldsymbol{v}}=\boldsymbol{R}\boldsymbol{v} . vˉ=Rv.

Thus, R(s)\boldsymbol{R}(s)R(s) defines the local reference frame

Xˉ=(xˉ,yˉ,zˉ) \bar{\mathcal X}=(\bar x,\bar y,\bar z) Xˉ=(xˉ,yˉ​,zˉ)

of each cross-section with respect to the global reference frame

X=(x,y,z). \mathcal X=(x,y,z). X=(x,y,z).

The xˉ\bar xxˉ-axis is tangent to the centroidal axis, while the yˉ\bar yyˉ​- and zˉ\bar zzˉ-axes coincide with the two central principal axes of the cross-section. Therefore, the geometry of the beam is completely characterised when the cross-section and the matrix R(s)\boldsymbol{R}(s)R(s) are known.

Let us consider two neighbouring cross-sections, ① and ②, separated by the infinitesimal distance dsdsds. Their relative rotation is infinitesimal and may therefore be represented by the vector

dφ={dφxˉdφyˉdφzˉ}. d\boldsymbol{\varphi} = \begin{Bmatrix} d\varphi_{\bar x}\\ d\varphi_{\bar y}\\ d\varphi_{\bar z} \end{Bmatrix}. dφ=⎩⎨⎧​dφxˉ​dφyˉ​​dφzˉ​​⎭⎬⎫​.

κxˉ=dφxˉds,κyˉ=dφyˉds,κzˉ=dφzˉds. \kappa_{\bar x}=\frac{d\varphi_{\bar x}}{ds}, \qquad \kappa_{\bar y}=\frac{d\varphi_{\bar y}}{ds}, \qquad \kappa_{\bar z}=\frac{d\varphi_{\bar z}}{ds}. κxˉ​=dsdφxˉ​​,κyˉ​​=dsdφyˉ​​​,κzˉ​=dsdφzˉ​​.

The curvature components may also be collected in the vector:

κ={κxˉκyˉκzˉ}. \boldsymbol{\kappa} = \begin{Bmatrix} \kappa_{\bar x}\\ \kappa_{\bar y}\\ \kappa_{\bar z} \end{Bmatrix}. κ=⎩⎨⎧​κxˉ​κyˉ​​κzˉ​​⎭⎬⎫​.

On each cross-section act six internal resultants: the axial force NNN, the two shear forces TyˉT_{\bar y}Tyˉ​​ and TzˉT_{\bar z}Tzˉ​, the torque MxˉM_{\bar x}Mxˉ​, and the two bending moments MyˉM_{\bar y}Myˉ​​ and MzˉM_{\bar z}Mzˉ​.

They are conveniently grouped into the local force and moment vectors:

Fˉ={NTyˉTzˉ},Mˉ={MxˉMyˉMzˉ}. \bar{\boldsymbol{F}} = \begin{Bmatrix} N\\ T_{\bar y}\\ T_{\bar z} \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \bar{\boldsymbol{M}} = \begin{Bmatrix} M_{\bar x}\\ M_{\bar y}\\ M_{\bar z} \end{Bmatrix}. Fˉ=⎩⎨⎧​NTyˉ​​Tzˉ​​⎭⎬⎫​,Mˉ=⎩⎨⎧​Mxˉ​Myˉ​​Mzˉ​​⎭⎬⎫​.

The corresponding vectors in the global reference frame are

F={FxFyFz},M={MxMyMz}, \boldsymbol{F}= \begin{Bmatrix} F_x\\ F_y\\ F_z \end{Bmatrix}, \qquad \boldsymbol{M}= \begin{Bmatrix} M_x\\ M_y\\ M_z \end{Bmatrix}, F=⎩⎨⎧​Fx​Fy​Fz​​⎭⎬⎫​,M=⎩⎨⎧​Mx​My​Mz​​⎭⎬⎫​,

and, according to the adopted passive transformation,

Fˉ=RF,Mˉ=RM. \boxed{ \bar{\boldsymbol{F}}=\boldsymbol{R}\boldsymbol{F}, \qquad \bar{\boldsymbol{M}}=\boldsymbol{R}\boldsymbol{M}. } Fˉ=RF,Mˉ=RM.​

Consider now an infinitesimal beam element of length dsdsds. Let q\boldsymbol{q}q denote the distributed force per unit length, expressed in the global reference frame, and let

qˉ=Rq \bar{\boldsymbol{q}}=\boldsymbol{R}\boldsymbol{q} qˉ​=Rq

be the corresponding local vector.

Let t\boldsymbol{t}t be the unit tangent to the centroidal axis, oriented in the direction of increasing sss. In the local frame,

Rt={100}. \boldsymbol{R}\boldsymbol{t}= \begin{Bmatrix} 1\\ 0\\ 0 \end{Bmatrix}. Rt=⎩⎨⎧​100​⎭⎬⎫​.

dFds=−q \frac{d\boldsymbol{F}}{ds}=-\boldsymbol{q} dsdF​=−q

These two vector equations are the differential equilibrium equations of a 3D slender curved beam.

For a 2D curved beam lying in the (xˉ,yˉ)(\bar x,\bar y)(xˉ,yˉ​) plane,

κxˉ=0,κyˉ=0,κzˉ=1r, \kappa_{\bar x}=0, \qquad \kappa_{\bar y}=0, \qquad \kappa_{\bar z}=\frac{1}{r}, κxˉ​=0,κyˉ​​=0,κzˉ​=r1​,

dMzˉds=−Tyˉ. \frac{dM_{\bar z}}{ds} = -T_{\bar y}. dsdMzˉ​​=−Tyˉ​​.

For a straight beam,

κxˉ=κyˉ=κzˉ=0, \kappa_{\bar x} = \kappa_{\bar y} = \kappa_{\bar z} = 0, κxˉ​=κyˉ​​=κzˉ​=0,

and the well-known differential equilibrium equations are recovered:

dNdx=−qx,dTydx=−qy,dMzdx=−Ty. \frac{dN}{dx}=-q_x, \qquad \frac{dT_y}{dx}=-q_y, \qquad \frac{dM_z}{dx}=-T_y. dxdN​=−qx​,dxdTy​​=−qy​,dxdMz​​=−Ty​.

The differential equilibrium equations derived above are generally valid. In the case of 3D beams undergoing large displacements, they must be formulated in the current deformed configuration.

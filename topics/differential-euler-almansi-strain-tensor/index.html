01

Starting point

This supplementary derivation gives the ordinary differential of the Euler–Almansi strain tensor used in Section 31.1, Subsection 05. The initial coordinates are x\boldsymbol{x}x, and the current coordinates are x^\widehat{\boldsymbol{x}}x. The Euler–Almansi strain tensor is:

e=12(I−F−TF−1)\boldsymbol e=\frac12\left(\boldsymbol I-\boldsymbol F^{-T}\boldsymbol F^{-1}\right)e=21​(I−F−TF−1)

For an infinitesimal additional displacement dud\boldsymbol udu, define its gradient with respect to the current coordinates:

g^=∂(du)∂x^\widehat{\boldsymbol g}=\frac{\partial(d\boldsymbol u)}{\partial\widehat{\boldsymbol x}}g​=∂x∂(du)​

The corresponding change in the deformation gradient is:

dF=g^Fd\boldsymbol F=\widehat{\boldsymbol g}\boldsymbol FdF=g​F

Introduce the symmetric tensor:

B=F−TF−1=I−2e\boldsymbol B=\boldsymbol F^{-T}\boldsymbol F^{-1}=\boldsymbol I-2\boldsymbol eB=F−TF−1=I−2e

Differential of an inverse matrix

For an invertible matrix A\boldsymbol AA, differentiate the identity:

AA−1=I\boldsymbol A\boldsymbol A^{-1}=\boldsymbol IAA−1=I

The product rule gives:

(dA)A−1+A d(A−1)=0(d\boldsymbol A)\boldsymbol A^{-1}+\boldsymbol A\,d(\boldsymbol A^{-1})=\boldsymbol 0(dA)A−1+Ad(A−1)=0

d(A−1)=−A−1(dA)A−1d(\boldsymbol A^{-1})=-\boldsymbol A^{-1}(d\boldsymbol A)\boldsymbol A^{-1}d(A−1)=−A−1(dA)A−1

Applying this result to F\boldsymbol FF and substituting dF=g^Fd\boldsymbol F=\widehat{\boldsymbol g}\boldsymbol FdF=g​F:

d(F−1)=−F−1(dF)F−1=−F−1g^d(\boldsymbol F^{-1})=-\boldsymbol F^{-1}(d\boldsymbol F)\boldsymbol F^{-1}=-\boldsymbol F^{-1}\widehat{\boldsymbol g}d(F−1)=−F−1(dF)F−1=−F−1g​

03

Differential of the Euler–Almansi strain tensor

Differentiate B=F−TF−1\boldsymbol B=\boldsymbol F^{-T}\boldsymbol F^{-1}B=F−TF−1 using the product rule:

dB=d(F−T)F−1+F−Td(F−1)d\boldsymbol B=d(\boldsymbol F^{-T})\boldsymbol F^{-1}+\boldsymbol F^{-T}d(\boldsymbol F^{-1})dB=d(F−T)F−1+F−Td(F−1)

dB=−g^TF−TF−1−F−TF−1g^=−g^TB−Bg^d\boldsymbol B=-\widehat{\boldsymbol g}^{T}\boldsymbol F^{-T}\boldsymbol F^{-1}-\boldsymbol F^{-T}\boldsymbol F^{-1}\widehat{\boldsymbol g}=-\widehat{\boldsymbol g}^{T}\boldsymbol B-\boldsymbol B\widehat{\boldsymbol g}dB=−g​TF−TF−1−F−TF−1g​=−g​TB−Bg​

dεsp=12(g^+g^T)d\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{sp}=\frac12\left(\widehat{\boldsymbol g}+\widehat{\boldsymbol g}^{T}\right)dεsp​=21​(g​+g​T)

The ordinary differential ded\boldsymbol ede is thus not generally equal to the infinitesimal spatial strain increment. The two additional terms reflect the changing current configuration. Their role in the internal mechanical work is explained in Section 31.1, Subsection 05.

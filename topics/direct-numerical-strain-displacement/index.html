01

From total strain to equilibrium

StrainSmall
DisplacementLarge
MaterialLinear elastic
Stress statePlane stress

Section 17.4 evaluates the total Hencky or Biot strain directly from the initial configuration to the current trial configuration. Its element internal nodal force vector fel\boldsymbol{f}_{\rm el}fel​ nevertheless retains the approximate strain–displacement matrix B\boldsymbol{B}B derived for the incremental formulation. The present section evaluates B\boldsymbol{B}B numerically from the total strain itself.

The idea is related to the direct total-strain evaluation introduced in Section 15.3. Here it is applied to Hencky and Biot strains, and numerical differentiation is used at two successive levels: first for B\boldsymbol{B}B, then for the tangent stiffness matrix kel\boldsymbol{k}_{\rm el}kel​. The central sequence is:

e⟶B⟶fel⟶kel\boxed{\boldsymbol{e}\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{B}\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{f}_{\rm el}\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{k}_{\rm el}}e⟶B⟶fel​⟶kel​​

This eliminates the need to use the incremental strain–displacement matrix of the previous formulations. In Section 17.5, B\boldsymbol{B}B is obtained directly by numerical differentiation of the total strain evaluated from the initial configuration. The resulting formulation is therefore more kinematically consistent, while the small-strain constitutive and thickness assumptions adopted in this chapter are retained.

02

Initial and current configurations

Sections 17.2 and 17.3 are incremental UL formulations. Sections 17.4 and 17.5 originate from those developments, but all total kinematic quantities are evaluated with respect to the initial configuration. Therefore, they should not be regarded as strictly Updated Lagrangian formulations. The load steps are retained as a continuation strategy for the nonlinear solution rather than as successive kinematic reference configurations.

The initial configuration is the undeformed geometry; the current configuration is the deformed geometry. During a Newton iteration, the current trial configuration has not yet converged. For the Q4 element, the initial and current trial Jacobian matrices give:

Ftot=(J0−1Jt)T\boxed{\boldsymbol{F}_{\rm tot}=\left(\boldsymbol{J}_0^{-1}\boldsymbol{J}_t\right)^T}Ftot​=(J0−1​Jt​)T​

Here, J0\boldsymbol{J}_0J0​ refers to the initial configuration, and Jt\boldsymbol{J}_tJt​ to the current trial configuration. No previously accumulated strain is required. The polar decomposition is:

Ftot=RtotUtot\boldsymbol{F}_{\rm tot}=\boldsymbol{R}_{\rm tot}\boldsymbol{U}_{\rm tot}Ftot​=Rtot​Utot​

The total rotation Rtot\boldsymbol{R}_{\rm tot}Rtot​ takes the initial orientation to the current trial orientation. It may be large even when the mechanical strains are small. The right stretch tensor Utot\boldsymbol{U}_{\rm tot}Utot​ determines the Hencky or Biot strain referred to the initial configuration:

EH=ln⁡UtotEB=Utot−I2\boldsymbol{E}_H=\ln\boldsymbol{U}_{\rm tot}\qquad\boldsymbol{E}_B=\boldsymbol{U}_{\rm tot}-\boldsymbol{I}_2EH​=lnUtot​EB​=Utot​−I2​

The tensor is then rotated to the current orientation:

e=RtotERtotT\boxed{\boldsymbol{e}=\boldsymbol{R}_{\rm tot}\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{R}_{\rm tot}^{T}}e=Rtot​ERtotT​​

In the program, Ftot, U, and R contain these total quantities. In Sections 17.2 and 17.3 the kinematics are incremental, so the rotation plays a different role: it relates successive configurations, rather than being obtained directly from the initial geometry.

In Sections 17.2–17.5, Hencky strains are used together with Cauchy stresses under the assumption of small mechanical strains. This combination is part of the adopted approximation and has proved sufficiently accurate for the examples considered here.

03

Spectral evaluation of the stretch and strain

The subroutine strain_el forms the current trial nodal coordinates from the initial coordinates and the total trial nodal displacements. It then calculates Ftot\boldsymbol{F}_{\rm tot}Ftot​ and the right Cauchy–Green tensor:

C=FtotTFtot\boldsymbol{C}=\boldsymbol{F}_{\rm tot}^{T}\boldsymbol{F}_{\rm tot}C=FtotT​Ftot​

Repeated calls to sqrtm and logm are expensive in the nested numerical procedure. For the present symmetric positive-definite 2×22\times22×2 tensor, an eigendecomposition gives a mathematically equivalent and considerably faster evaluation:

C=QΛQT\boldsymbol{C}=\boldsymbol{Q}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{Q}^{T}C=QΛQT

The columns of Q\boldsymbol{Q}Q are orthonormal eigenvectors. The principal stretches are the square roots of the eigenvalues of C\boldsymbol{C}C:

λi=Λii\lambda_i=\sqrt{\Lambda_{ii}}λi​=Λii​​

Consequently:

Utot=Qdiag⁡(λi)QT\boldsymbol{U}_{\rm tot}=\boldsymbol{Q}\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)\boldsymbol{Q}^{T}Utot​=Qdiag(λi​)QT
ln⁡Utot=Qdiag⁡(ln⁡λi)QT\ln\boldsymbol{U}_{\rm tot}=\boldsymbol{Q}\operatorname{diag}(\ln\lambda_i)\boldsymbol{Q}^{T}lnUtot​=Qdiag(lnλi​)QT

The sequence [Q,L]=eig(C); la=sqrt(diag(L)); U=Q*diag(la)*Q'; replaces U=sqrtm(C);. For Hencky strain, the same eigendecomposition also replaces EE=logm(U); by EE=Q*diag(log(la))*Q';. The implementation is:

MATLAB — strain_el
[Q,L]=eig(C);
la=sqrt(diag(L));
U=Q*diag(la)*Q';
R=Ftot/U;

if istrain==1
    EE=Q*diag(log(la))*Q';
else
    EE=U-eye(2);
end

ee=R*EE*R';
estr=[ee(1,1); ee(2,2); 2*ee(1,2)];

Set istrain=1 in stiff.m for Hencky strain or istrain=2 for Biot strain. The engineering shear component returned in estr is twice the off-diagonal tensor component.

04

Numerical strain–displacement matrix

For the finite-element calculation, the components of e\boldsymbol{e}e are collected in engineering notation:

ε={exxeyy2exy}={exeyγxy}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=\begin{Bmatrix}e_{xx}\\e_{yy}\\2e_{xy}\end{Bmatrix}=\begin{Bmatrix}e_x\\e_y\\\gamma_{xy}\end{Bmatrix}ε=⎩⎨⎧​exx​eyy​2exy​​⎭⎬⎫​=⎩⎨⎧​ex​ey​γxy​​⎭⎬⎫​

The total element nodal-displacement vector has eight components:

uel=(u1  v1  u2  v2  u3  v3  u4  v4)T\boldsymbol{u}_{\rm el}=\left(u_1\;v_1\;u_2\;v_2\;u_3\;v_3\;u_4\;v_4\right)^Tuel​=(u1​v1​u2​v2​u3​v3​u4​v4​)T

The 3×83\times83×8 strain–displacement matrix is the derivative of these engineering strain components:

B=∂ε∂uel\boldsymbol{B}=\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}{\partial\boldsymbol{u}_{\rm el}}B=∂uel​∂ε​

Let ci\boldsymbol{c}_ici​ be the eight-component unit vector with a one in position iii and zeros elsewhere. Thus, δBci\delta_B\boldsymbol{c}_iδB​ci​ is a displacement perturbation, not a strain increment. Column iii is evaluated by central differences:

B(:,i)≈ε(uel+δBci)−ε(uel−δBci)2δB\boxed{\boldsymbol{B}(:,i)\approx\frac{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{\rm el}+\delta_B\boldsymbol{c}_i\right)-\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{\rm el}-\delta_B\boldsymbol{c}_i\right)}{2\delta_B}}B(:,i)≈2δB​ε(uel​+δB​ci​)−ε(uel​−δB​ci​)​​

Each perturbation requires a fresh evaluation of the total deformation gradient, stretch, rotation, and strain. The variable ddB represents δB\delta_BδB​. The unperturbed strain and total rotation are saved before the perturbations:

MATLAB — numerical B in fel_el
sels=sel;
strain_el
e0=estr;
R0=R;

B=zeros(3,8);
for ib=1:8
    sels=sel;
    sels(ib)=sels(ib)+ddB;
    strain_el
    ep=estr;

    sels=sel;
    sels(ib)=sels(ib)-ddB;
    strain_el
    em=estr;

    B(:,ib)=(ep-em)/(2*ddB);
end

The vector S in main.m contains total trial nodal displacements. The vector dS is a Newton displacement correction; neither is a strain tensor. The program retains S between load steps, while St stores the converged total displacements.

05

Element internal force and numerical tangent

The unperturbed engineering strain vector determines the Cauchy stress components through the plane-stress constitutive matrix D\boldsymbol{D}D:

σ=Dε\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}σ=Dε

The implemented element internal nodal force vector is evaluated at the four Gauss points using the initial Jacobian determinant and the current-thickness approximation:

fel=∑G=14hcur,GBGTσG∣det⁡J0,G∣\boldsymbol{f}_{\rm el}=\sum_{G=1}^{4}h_{{\rm cur},G}\boldsymbol{B}_G^T\boldsymbol{\sigma}_G\left|\det\boldsymbol{J}_{0,G}\right|fel​=G=1∑4​hcur,G​BGT​σG​∣detJ0,G​∣
hcur≈[1−ν1−ν(ex+ey)]hh_{\rm cur}\approx\left[1-\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}(e_x+e_y)\right]hhcur​≈[1−1−νν​(ex​+ey​)]h

Here, hhh is the initial thickness. This thickness relation is a small-strain approximation; it is not a general exact relation for finite elastic strains. The expression above describes the implemented fel\boldsymbol{f}_{\rm el}fel​, including its adopted integration measure and constitutive assumptions.

As in Section 16.3 and Section 17.4, the tangent stiffness matrix is obtained by numerical differentiation of fel\boldsymbol{f}_{\rm el}fel​:

kel=∂fel∂uel\boldsymbol{k}_{\rm el}=\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{f}_{\rm el}}{\partial\boldsymbol{u}_{\rm el}}kel​=∂uel​∂fel​​
kel(:,i)≈fel(uel+δKci)−fel(uel−δKci)2δK\boxed{\boldsymbol{k}_{\rm el}(:,i)\approx\frac{\boldsymbol{f}_{\rm el}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{\rm el}+\delta_K\boldsymbol{c}_i\right)-\boldsymbol{f}_{\rm el}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{\rm el}-\delta_K\boldsymbol{c}_i\right)}{2\delta_K}}kel​(:,i)≈2δK​fel​(uel​+δK​ci​)−fel​(uel​−δK​ci​)​​

The program uses ddB=1e-5 and ddK=1e-5. For each element, one unperturbed and sixteen perturbed evaluations of fel\boldsymbol{f}_{\rm el}fel​ are performed. Within each evaluation, B\boldsymbol{B}B is recalculated numerically at every Gauss point. This nested procedure is simple to follow but computationally expensive.

The flag isave ensures that only the unperturbed evaluation is stored for post-processing. Artificial symmetrization of kel\boldsymbol{k}_{\rm el}kel​ is not introduced.

06

Global and local Cauchy stresses

The global frame is the fixed x−yx-yx−y frame. The local frame follows the current element orientation represented, at each Gauss point, by the total polar rotation. The global Cauchy stress tensor is:

σg=[σxτxyτxyσy]\boldsymbol{\sigma}_g=\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_x&\tau_{xy}\\\tau_{xy}&\sigma_y\end{bmatrix}σg​=[σx​τxy​​τxy​σy​​]

The local components are:

σl=RtotTσgRtot\boxed{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_l=\boldsymbol{R}_{\rm tot}^T\boldsymbol{\sigma}_g\boldsymbol{R}_{\rm tot}}σl​=RtotT​σg​Rtot​​

This option is possible because the total rotation is explicitly available in Sections 17.4 and 17.5. The same direct construction is not available in Sections 17.2 and 17.3, where the rotations are incremental; an additional reconstruction of the total rotation would be needed.

In fel_el, R0 holds the unperturbed total rotation. It is used for the stress transformation after B\boldsymbol{B}B has been calculated, because the variable R is overwritten during the perturbations:

MATLAB — Gauss-point stress storage
sig=e0'*DHooke;
sg=[sig(1) sig(3)
    sig(3) sig(2)];
sl=R0'*sg*R0;

sigmt(ig,1:3,istep+1)=sig;
sigmt(ig,5:7,istep+1)=[sl(1,1) sl(2,2) sl(1,2)];

Columns 1–3 of sigmt store global σx,σy,τxy\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\tau_{xy}σx​,σy​,τxy​; column 4 stores von Mises stress; columns 5–7 store the corresponding local components. The local stresses are calculated at the Gauss points before extrapolation and nodal averaging.

The plot_stress routine asks for the global or local frame when a stress component is selected. Von Mises stress uses the same stored value in either frame because it is invariant under orthogonal rotation.

09

Conclusions

  1. Total Hencky or Biot strain is evaluated directly from the initial configuration to the current trial configuration. Load stepping is a continuation strategy, not a sequence of kinematic reference updates.
  2. The total rotation expresses the strain in the current orientation and also permits global and local Cauchy stress components to be calculated before post-processing.
  3. The matrix B\boldsymbol{B}B is calculated by numerical differentiation of the total engineering strain components. The vector fel\boldsymbol{f}_{\rm el}fel​ is then evaluated, and its numerical derivative gives kel\boldsymbol{k}_{\rm el}kel​. This improves the consistency of the implemented numerical procedure, without removing its constitutive and thickness assumptions.

The results also show that the improved formulation of Section 17.3 is sufficiently reliable for the class of problems considered here. Although its strain update contains approximations, the iterative Newton procedure largely compensates for their effect in the final converged solution. This is an observation for the examples considered, not an exact automatic cancellation of approximation errors. The developments of Sections 17.4 and 17.5 improve the consistency of the algorithm, but this progress is obtained at the price of rapidly increasing theoretical and computational complexity.

The present MATLAB implementations are written sequentially for clarity. However, many of the independent perturbation evaluations required for the numerical calculation of B\boldsymbol{B}B and kel\boldsymbol{k}_{\rm el}kel​ can be performed simultaneously. MATLAB parallel-computing capabilities can therefore reduce the execution time substantially, particularly for the algorithms of Sections 17.4 and 17.5.

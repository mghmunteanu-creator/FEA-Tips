Elastic–perfectly plastic behavior

StrainSmall
DisplacementLarge
MaterialElastic–perfectly plastic

This section considers an elastic–perfectly plastic material: after yielding, plastic strain can increase under constant uniaxial stress σ0\sigma_0σ0​, the yield stress. The direct and consistent Updated Lagrangian formulation developed in Section 27.2 is adopted.

Material nonlinearity arises from the nonlinear relationship between stress and strain. Plasticity is the ability of a material to undergo permanent deformation, that is, an irreversible change of shape under an applied load [1].

Only rate-independent plasticity is considered here. It is characterized by irreversible straining when a certain stress level is reached. Plastic strains are assumed to develop instantaneously, independently of time [1].

Loading and unloading are shown below. Unloading is linear and parallel to the initial linear-elastic branch, as described in [1].

03

Associated flow rule

The flow rule determines the direction of plastic straining. For associated plasticity, it is given by [1], [2]:

dεpl=dλ∂F∂σordεpl=dλ∇Fd\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{pl}=d\lambda\frac{\partial F}{\partial\boldsymbol\sigma}\qquad\text{or}\qquad d\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{pl}=d\lambda\nabla Fdεpl​=dλ∂σ∂F​ordεpl​=dλ∇F

Here, dλd\lambdadλ is the plastic multiplier and FFF is the yield function. This is also called the normality rule: the plastic strain increment is normal to the yield surface.

The engineering stress and strain vectors are:

σ={σxσyσzτxyτyzτzx}ε={εxεyεzγxyγyzγzx}\boldsymbol\sigma=\begin{Bmatrix}\sigma_x\\\sigma_y\\\sigma_z\\\tau_{xy}\\\tau_{yz}\\\tau_{zx}\end{Bmatrix}\qquad\boldsymbol\varepsilon=\begin{Bmatrix}\varepsilon_x\\\varepsilon_y\\\varepsilon_z\\\gamma_{xy}\\\gamma_{yz}\\\gamma_{zx}\end{Bmatrix}σ=⎩⎨⎧​σx​σy​σz​τxy​τyz​τzx​​⎭⎬⎫​ε=⎩⎨⎧​εx​εy​εz​γxy​γyz​γzx​​⎭⎬⎫​

For an elastic–perfectly plastic material, there is no hardening. An infinitesimal strain increment is divided into an elastic part and a plastic part:

dε=dεe+dεpld\boldsymbol\varepsilon=d\boldsymbol\varepsilon_e+d\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{pl}dε=dεe​+dεpl​

The elastic and plastic parts are:

dεe=D−1dσdεpl=dλ∂F∂σd\boldsymbol\varepsilon_e=\boldsymbol D^{-1}d\boldsymbol\sigma\qquad d\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{pl}=d\lambda\frac{\partial F}{\partial\boldsymbol\sigma}dεe​=D−1dσdεpl​=dλ∂σ∂F​

Therefore:

dε=D−1dσ+dλ∂F∂σ(*)d\boldsymbol\varepsilon=\boldsymbol D^{-1}d\boldsymbol\sigma+d\lambda\frac{\partial F}{\partial\boldsymbol\sigma}\tag{*}dε=D−1dσ+dλ∂σ∂F​(*)

Plastic straining occurs only if the elastic trial stress increment:

dσ=D dεd\boldsymbol\sigma=\boldsymbol D\,d\boldsymbol\varepsilondσ=Ddε

tends to move the stress outside the yield surface. If the increment instead produces unloading, the response is elastic and no plastic strain increment occurs.

When the material is unloaded after plastic deformation, unloading follows a linear elastic path with the same Young's modulus EEE as the initial elastic loading branch. Consequently, the unloading line is parallel to the initial elastic branch, and a permanent plastic strain remains when the stress returns to zero, as illustrated in Figure 4.

05

Finite increments and stress drift

A first numerical approximation replaces infinitesimal increments by small but finite increments. At iteration jjj of the current load step:

Δσ=DepjΔεj\Delta\boldsymbol\sigma=\boldsymbol D_{epj}\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon_jΔσ=Depj​Δεj​

Here, Δσ\Delta\boldsymbol\sigmaΔσ is a finite increment, in contrast to the differential relation:

dσ=Dep dεd\boldsymbol\sigma=\boldsymbol D_{ep}\,d\boldsymbol\varepsilondσ=Dep​dε

In the direct UL implementation, the current total Euler–Almansi strain is evaluated from the initial and current configurations, as in Section 27.2. The strain increment supplied to the constitutive algorithm is the difference between this current total strain and the strain stored at the preceding converged load step.

For the explicit approximation, the elastoplastic matrix is evaluated at the known stress:

Depj=Dep(σj)\boldsymbol D_{epj}=\boldsymbol D_{ep}(\boldsymbol\sigma_j)Depj​=Dep​(σj​)

The stress update would then be:

σj+1=σj+Δσ\boldsymbol\sigma_{j+1}=\boldsymbol\sigma_j+\Delta\boldsymbol\sigmaσj+1​=σj​+Δσ

Smaller increments reduce the error of this approximation, but accumulated error can cause the stress to drift away from the yield surface and violate the yield condition, as illustrated below [2].

Replacing the differentials in (*) by finite increments gives:

Δε=D−1Δσ+Δλ∇F(σj)\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon=\boldsymbol D^{-1}\Delta\boldsymbol\sigma+\Delta\lambda\nabla F(\boldsymbol\sigma_j)Δε=D−1Δσ+Δλ∇F(σj​)

Or, in terms of stress increments:

Δσ=DΔε−ΔλD∇F(σj)\Delta\boldsymbol\sigma=\boldsymbol D\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon-\Delta\lambda\boldsymbol D\nabla F(\boldsymbol\sigma_j)Δσ=DΔε−ΔλD∇F(σj​)

The drift is avoided by evaluating the plastic-flow direction at the corrected stress and enforcing the yield condition there. The implicit equation is:

Δσ=DΔε−ΔλD∇F(σj+Δσ)\Delta\boldsymbol\sigma=\boldsymbol D\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon-\Delta\lambda\boldsymbol D\nabla F(\boldsymbol\sigma_j+\Delta\boldsymbol\sigma)Δσ=DΔε−ΔλD∇F(σj​+Δσ)

For this local correction, σj\boldsymbol\sigma_jσj​ denotes the known starting stress. In stiff_cons.m, it is sig0, taken from the preceding converged load step. It is not accumulated from one global Newton iteration to the next.

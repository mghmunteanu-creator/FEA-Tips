Current-configuration kinematics

Another finite-strain measure is presented here in a form parallel to the Green–Lagrange strain tensor introduced in Chapter 10. Green–Lagrange strain measures changes in length with respect to the initial configuration, whereas Euler–Almansi strain measures them with respect to the current configuration.

The initial and current coordinates of a material point are:

x={xy},x^={x^y^}=x+u,u={uv}\boldsymbol{x}=\begin{Bmatrix}x\\[3pt]y\end{Bmatrix},\qquad \hat{\boldsymbol{x}}=\begin{Bmatrix}\hat{x}\\[3pt]\hat{y}\end{Bmatrix}=\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{u},\qquad \boldsymbol{u}=\begin{Bmatrix}u\\[3pt]v\end{Bmatrix}x={xy​},x^={x^y^​​}=x+u,u={uv​}

04

Small-strain comparison

For very small strains:

EGL≈eEA≈εE_{GL}\approx e_{EA}\approx\varepsilonEGL​≈eEA​≈ε

For steel, for example, elastic strain is usually smaller than approximately 0.010.010.01, provided that no plasticity occurs.

Over the interval:

ε∈[−0.05,0.05]\varepsilon\in[-0.05,0.05]ε∈[−0.05,0.05]

the difference from engineering strain is approximately 2–3%2\text{–}3\%2–3% for Green–Lagrange strain and approximately 7–8%7\text{–}8\%7–8% for Euler–Almansi strain.

Current-configuration stress interpretation

When the Euler–Almansi strains are small, the usual linear elastic Hooke law can be applied in the current configuration, providing a very good approximation of the Cauchy stresses.

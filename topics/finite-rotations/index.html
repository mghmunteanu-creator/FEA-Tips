01

Finite rotations, 2D case

Consider a vector v0\boldsymbol v_0v0​, having length vvv, and rotate it by an angle φ\varphiφ about the zzz-axis (the axis perpendicular to the plane). This gives the vector v\boldsymbol vv.

We can write successively:

v0={vcos⁡αvsin⁡α}\boldsymbol v_0=\begin{Bmatrix}v\cos\alpha\\v\sin\alpha\end{Bmatrix}v0​={vcosαvsinα​}
v={vcos⁡(α+φ)vsin⁡(α+φ)}={v(cos⁡αcos⁡φ−sin⁡αsin⁡φ)v(cos⁡αsin⁡φ+sin⁡αcos⁡φ)}=[cos⁡φ−sin⁡φsin⁡φcos⁡φ]⏟R{vcos⁡αvsin⁡α}⏟v0.\begin{aligned}\boldsymbol v&=\begin{Bmatrix}v\cos(\alpha+\varphi)\\v\sin(\alpha+\varphi)\end{Bmatrix}\\&=\begin{Bmatrix}v(\cos\alpha\cos\varphi-\sin\alpha\sin\varphi)\\v(\cos\alpha\sin\varphi+\sin\alpha\cos\varphi)\end{Bmatrix}\\&=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\cos\varphi&-\sin\varphi\\\sin\varphi&\cos\varphi\end{bmatrix}}_{\boldsymbol R}\underbrace{\begin{Bmatrix}v\cos\alpha\\v\sin\alpha\end{Bmatrix}}_{\boldsymbol v_0}.\end{aligned}v​={vcos(α+φ)vsin(α+φ)​}={v(cosαcosφ−sinαsinφ)v(cosαsinφ+sinαcosφ)​}=R[cosφsinφ​−sinφcosφ​]​​v0​{vcosαvsinα​}​​.​

And finally:

v=Rv0,R=[cos⁡φ−sin⁡φsin⁡φcos⁡φ].\boldsymbol v=\boldsymbol R\boldsymbol v_0,\qquad\boldsymbol R=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\varphi&-\sin\varphi\\\sin\varphi&\cos\varphi\end{bmatrix}.v=Rv0​,R=[cosφsinφ​−sinφcosφ​].

If the angle φ\varphiφ is very small, we can write, considering that cos⁡φ≈1\cos\varphi\approx1cosφ≈1 and sin⁡φ≈φ\sin\varphi\approx\varphisinφ≈φ:

R≈[1−φφ1].\boldsymbol R\approx\begin{bmatrix}1&-\varphi\\\varphi&1\end{bmatrix}.R≈[1φ​−φ1​].

02

Infinitesimal rotations

A rotation is represented by a magnitude, the angle of rotation, and a direction, the axis of rotation. Let’s consider the magnitude φ\varphiφ and the axis defined by the unit vector n\boldsymbol nn.

For an infinitesimal rotation, the rotation can be represented by a vector:

φ=φn,orφ={φxφyφz}.\boldsymbol\varphi=\varphi\boldsymbol n,\qquad\text{or}\qquad\boldsymbol\varphi=\begin{Bmatrix}\varphi_x\\\varphi_y\\\varphi_z\end{Bmatrix}.φ=φn,orφ=⎩⎨⎧​φx​φy​φz​​⎭⎬⎫​.

And:

φ=∥φ∥=φx2+φy2+φz2.\varphi=\lVert\boldsymbol\varphi\rVert=\sqrt{\varphi_x^2+\varphi_y^2+\varphi_z^2}.φ=∥φ∥=φx2​+φy2​+φz2​​.

The components of rotation, φx\varphi_xφx​, φy\varphi_yφy​, φz\varphi_zφz​, are very small and are treated as infinitesimal values. The corresponding rotation matrix is:

R=RzRyRx=[1−φz0φz10001][10φy010−φy01][10001−φx0φx1]⟶R=[1−φzφyφz1−φx−φyφx1].\begin{aligned}\boldsymbol R&=\boldsymbol R_z\boldsymbol R_y\boldsymbol R_x\\&=\begin{bmatrix}1&-\varphi_z&0\\\varphi_z&1&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\varphi_y\\0&1&0\\-\varphi_y&0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&-\varphi_x\\0&\varphi_x&1\end{bmatrix}\\&\longrightarrow\quad\boldsymbol R=\begin{bmatrix}1&-\varphi_z&\varphi_y\\\varphi_z&1&-\varphi_x\\-\varphi_y&\varphi_x&1\end{bmatrix}.\end{aligned}R​=Rz​Ry​Rx​=​1φz​0​−φz​10​001​​​10−φy​​010​φy​01​​​100​01φx​​0−φx​1​​⟶R=​1φz​−φy​​−φz​1φx​​φy​−φx​1​​.​

The terms of the second and third degrees were neglected, and it was assumed that:

sin⁡φ≃φ,cos⁡φ≃1.\sin\varphi\simeq\varphi,\qquad\cos\varphi\simeq1.sinφ≃φ,cosφ≃1.

In fact, the rotation φ\boldsymbol\varphiφ is the resultant of three rotations, the vector sum of three vectors:

φ={φxφyφz}={φx00}+{0φy0}+{00φz}.\boldsymbol\varphi=\begin{Bmatrix}\varphi_x\\\varphi_y\\\varphi_z\end{Bmatrix}=\begin{Bmatrix}\varphi_x\\0\\0\end{Bmatrix}+\begin{Bmatrix}0\\\varphi_y\\0\end{Bmatrix}+\begin{Bmatrix}0\\0\\\varphi_z\end{Bmatrix}.φ=⎩⎨⎧​φx​φy​φz​​⎭⎬⎫​=⎩⎨⎧​φx​00​⎭⎬⎫​+⎩⎨⎧​0φy​0​⎭⎬⎫​+⎩⎨⎧​00φz​​⎭⎬⎫​.

Composition of small (infinitesimal) rotations is commutative!

03

Finite rotations

Let’s find the rotation matrix R\boldsymbol RR if the rotation angle is finite. In this case, finite rotations do not follow the parallelogram law of vector addition. Moreover, the composition of rotations is non-commutative.

The problem: consider a vector v0\boldsymbol v_0v0​ and rotate it by an angle φ\varphiφ around the axis having the direction of the unit vector n\boldsymbol nn; the new position of the vector is v\boldsymbol vv. We have to find the matrix R\boldsymbol RR, [1], [2], [3], [4]:

v=R(φ)v0.\boldsymbol v=\boldsymbol R(\varphi)\boldsymbol v_0.v=R(φ)v0​.

This is called an active rotation: the vector is rotated while the reference frame remains fixed.

Vector sum:

v=v0+Δv.\boldsymbol v=\boldsymbol v_0+\Delta\boldsymbol v.v=v0​+Δv.
Δv=PD‾+DQ‾⟶v=v0+PD‾+DQ‾.\Delta\boldsymbol v=\overline{PD}+\overline{DQ}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\boldsymbol v=\boldsymbol v_0+\overline{PD}+\overline{DQ}.Δv=PD+DQ​⟶v=v0​+PD+DQ​.

Vector DQ‾\overline{DQ}DQ​ has the direction of the cross product n×v0\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0n×v0​ because DQ‾\overline{DQ}DQ​ is perpendicular to the plane (n,v0)(\boldsymbol n,\boldsymbol v_0)(n,v0​).

The modulus of n×v0\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0n×v0​ is:

∥n×v0∥=∥v∥sin⁡α⟶∥n×v0∥=a.\lVert\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0\rVert=\lVert\boldsymbol v\rVert\sin\alpha\quad\longrightarrow\quad\lVert\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0\rVert=a.∥n×v0​∥=∥v∥sinα⟶∥n×v0​∥=a.

As:

DQ=asin⁡φ,DQ=a\sin\varphi,DQ=asinφ,

we get:

DQ‾=(n×v0)sin⁡φ.\overline{DQ}=(\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0)\sin\varphi.DQ​=(n×v0​)sinφ.

Vector PD‾\overline{PD}PD. The modulus:

PD=a−acos⁡φ=2asin⁡2φ2.PD=a-a\cos\varphi=2a\sin^2\frac{\varphi}{2}.PD=a−acosφ=2asin22φ​.

Obviously, n\boldsymbol nn is perpendicular to n×v0\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0n×v0​, and thus n×(n×v0)\boldsymbol n\times(\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0)n×(n×v0​) has the direction of vector PD‾\overline{PD}PD. But:

∥n×(n×v0)∥=∥n×v0∥=a,\lVert\boldsymbol n\times(\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0)\rVert=\lVert\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0\rVert=a,∥n×(n×v0​)∥=∥n×v0​∥=a,

therefore:

PD‾=2(n×(n×v0))sin⁡2φ2.\overline{PD}=2\bigl(\boldsymbol n\times(\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0)\bigr)\sin^2\frac{\varphi}{2}.PD=2(n×(n×v0​))sin22φ​.

The relation between v\boldsymbol vv and v0\boldsymbol v_0v0​:

v=v0+(n×v0)sin⁡φ+2(n×(n×v0))sin⁡2φ2,\boldsymbol v=\boldsymbol v_0+(\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0)\sin\varphi+2\bigl(\boldsymbol n\times(\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0)\bigr)\sin^2\frac{\varphi}{2},v=v0​+(n×v0​)sinφ+2(n×(n×v0​))sin22φ​,

or:

v=v0+(n×v0)sin⁡φ+(n×(n×v0))(1−cos⁡φ).(*)\boldsymbol v=\boldsymbol v_0+(\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0)\sin\varphi+\bigl(\boldsymbol n\times(\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0)\bigr)(1-\cos\varphi).\tag{*}v=v0​+(n×v0​)sinφ+(n×(n×v0​))(1−cosφ).(*)

To convert this equation into matrix form, we introduce the notations:

n={nxnynz},nx2+ny2+nz2=1.\boldsymbol n=\begin{Bmatrix}n_x\\n_y\\n_z\end{Bmatrix},\qquad n_x^2+n_y^2+n_z^2=1.n=⎩⎨⎧​nx​ny​nz​​⎭⎬⎫​,nx2​+ny2​+nz2​=1.
n×v0 ⟶ [0−nznynz0−nx−nynx0]{v0xv0yv0z}.\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0\ \longrightarrow\ \begin{bmatrix}0&-n_z&n_y\\n_z&0&-n_x\\-n_y&n_x&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{Bmatrix}v_{0x}\\v_{0y}\\v_{0z}\end{Bmatrix}.n×v0​ ⟶ ​0nz​−ny​​−nz​0nx​​ny​−nx​0​​⎩⎨⎧​v0x​v0y​v0z​​⎭⎬⎫​.

Or:

n×v0 ⟶ n~v0,\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0\ \longrightarrow\ \widetilde{\boldsymbol n}\boldsymbol v_0,n×v0​ ⟶ nv0​,

with:

n~=[0−nznynz0−nx−nynx0].\widetilde{\boldsymbol n}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-n_z&n_y\\n_z&0&-n_x\\-n_y&n_x&0\end{bmatrix}.n=​0nz​−ny​​−nz​0nx​​ny​−nx​0​​.
n×(n×v0) ⟶ n~ 2v0,\boldsymbol n\times(\boldsymbol n\times\boldsymbol v_0)\ \longrightarrow\ \widetilde{\boldsymbol n}^{\,2}\boldsymbol v_0,n×(n×v0​) ⟶ n2v0​,

where:

n~ 2=n~n~=[−(ny2+nz2)nxnynznxnxny−(nz2+nx2)nynznznxnynz−(nx2+ny2)].\widetilde{\boldsymbol n}^{\,2}=\widetilde{\boldsymbol n}\widetilde{\boldsymbol n}=\begin{bmatrix}-(n_y^2+n_z^2)&n_xn_y&n_zn_x\\n_xn_y&-(n_z^2+n_x^2)&n_yn_z\\n_zn_x&n_yn_z&-(n_x^2+n_y^2)\end{bmatrix}.n2=nn=​−(ny2​+nz2​)nx​ny​nz​nx​​nx​ny​−(nz2​+nx2​)ny​nz​​nz​nx​ny​nz​−(nx2​+ny2​)​​.

This gives:

v=(E+sin⁡φ n~+(1−cos⁡φ)n~ 2)v0⟶v=Rv0.\boldsymbol v=\bigl(\boldsymbol E+\sin\varphi\,\widetilde{\boldsymbol n}+(1-\cos\varphi)\widetilde{\boldsymbol n}^{\,2}\bigr)\boldsymbol v_0\quad\longrightarrow\quad\boldsymbol v=\boldsymbol R\boldsymbol v_0.v=(E+sinφn+(1−cosφ)n2)v0​⟶v=Rv0​.

E\boldsymbol EE is the unit matrix. That is, equation (*) becomes:

R=E+sin⁡φ n~+(1−cos⁡φ)n~ 2.(**)\boldsymbol R=\boldsymbol E+\sin\varphi\,\widetilde{\boldsymbol n}+(1-\cos\varphi)\widetilde{\boldsymbol n}^{\,2}.\tag{**}R=E+sinφn+(1−cosφ)n2.(**)

Finally:

R=[cos⁡φ+nx2(1−cos⁡φ)nxny(1−cos⁡φ)−nzsin⁡φnznx(1−cos⁡φ)+nysin⁡φnxny(1−cos⁡φ)+nzsin⁡φcos⁡φ+ny2(1−cos⁡φ)nynz(1−cos⁡φ)−nxsin⁡φnznx(1−cos⁡φ)−nysin⁡φnynz(1−cos⁡φ)+nxsin⁡φcos⁡φ+nz2(1−cos⁡φ)].\boldsymbol R=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\varphi+n_x^2(1-\cos\varphi)&n_xn_y(1-\cos\varphi)-n_z\sin\varphi&n_zn_x(1-\cos\varphi)+n_y\sin\varphi\\n_xn_y(1-\cos\varphi)+n_z\sin\varphi&\cos\varphi+n_y^2(1-\cos\varphi)&n_yn_z(1-\cos\varphi)-n_x\sin\varphi\\n_zn_x(1-\cos\varphi)-n_y\sin\varphi&n_yn_z(1-\cos\varphi)+n_x\sin\varphi&\cos\varphi+n_z^2(1-\cos\varphi)\end{bmatrix}.R=​cosφ+nx2​(1−cosφ)nx​ny​(1−cosφ)+nz​sinφnz​nx​(1−cosφ)−ny​sinφ​nx​ny​(1−cosφ)−nz​sinφcosφ+ny2​(1−cosφ)ny​nz​(1−cosφ)+nx​sinφ​nz​nx​(1−cosφ)+ny​sinφny​nz​(1−cosφ)−nx​sinφcosφ+nz2​(1−cosφ)​​.

04

Another form of the rotation matrix

Now we introduce this vector:

ρ=φn={φxφyφz},\boldsymbol\rho=\varphi\boldsymbol n=\begin{Bmatrix}\varphi_x\\\varphi_y\\\varphi_z\end{Bmatrix},ρ=φn=⎩⎨⎧​φx​φy​φz​​⎭⎬⎫​,

and:

φ=φx2+φy2+φz2.\varphi=\sqrt{\varphi_x^2+\varphi_y^2+\varphi_z^2}.φ=φx2​+φy2​+φz2​​.

ρ\boldsymbol\rhoρ may be called a rotation vector or axis-angle vector. However, finite rotations do not compose by ordinary vector addition. Relation (**) between v\boldsymbol vv and v0\boldsymbol v_0v0​ becomes:

v=(E+sin⁡φφρ~+1−cos⁡φφ2ρ~ 2)v0.\boldsymbol v=\left(\boldsymbol E+\frac{\sin\varphi}{\varphi}\widetilde{\boldsymbol\rho}+\frac{1-\cos\varphi}{\varphi^2}\widetilde{\boldsymbol\rho}^{\,2}\right)\boldsymbol v_0.v=(E+φsinφ​ρ​+φ21−cosφ​ρ​2)v0​.

And finally:

R=E+sin⁡φφ[0−φzφyφz0−φx−φyφx0]+1−cos⁡φφ2[−(φy2+φz2)φxφyφzφxφxφy−(φz2+φx2)φyφzφzφxφyφz−(φx2+φy2)].\begin{aligned}\boldsymbol R={}&\boldsymbol E+\frac{\sin\varphi}{\varphi}\begin{bmatrix}0&-\varphi_z&\varphi_y\\\varphi_z&0&-\varphi_x\\-\varphi_y&\varphi_x&0\end{bmatrix}\\&+\frac{1-\cos\varphi}{\varphi^2}\begin{bmatrix}-(\varphi_y^2+\varphi_z^2)&\varphi_x\varphi_y&\varphi_z\varphi_x\\\varphi_x\varphi_y&-(\varphi_z^2+\varphi_x^2)&\varphi_y\varphi_z\\\varphi_z\varphi_x&\varphi_y\varphi_z&-(\varphi_x^2+\varphi_y^2)\end{bmatrix}.\end{aligned}R=​E+φsinφ​​0φz​−φy​​−φz​0φx​​φy​−φx​0​​+φ21−cosφ​​−(φy2​+φz2​)φx​φy​φz​φx​​φx​φy​−(φz2​+φx2​)φy​φz​​φz​φx​φy​φz​−(φx2​+φy2​)​​.​

Or:

R=E+sin⁡φ[0−nznynz0−nx−nynx0]+(1−cos⁡φ)[−(ny2+nz2)nxnynznxnxny−(nz2+nx2)nynznznxnynz−(nx2+ny2)].\begin{aligned}\boldsymbol R={}&\boldsymbol E+\sin\varphi\begin{bmatrix}0&-n_z&n_y\\n_z&0&-n_x\\-n_y&n_x&0\end{bmatrix}\\&+(1-\cos\varphi)\begin{bmatrix}-(n_y^2+n_z^2)&n_xn_y&n_zn_x\\n_xn_y&-(n_z^2+n_x^2)&n_yn_z\\n_zn_x&n_yn_z&-(n_x^2+n_y^2)\end{bmatrix}.\end{aligned}R=​E+sinφ​0nz​−ny​​−nz​0nx​​ny​−nx​0​​+(1−cosφ)​−(ny2​+nz2​)nx​ny​nz​nx​​nx​ny​−(nz2​+nx2​)ny​nz​​nz​nx​ny​nz​−(nx2​+ny2​)​​.​

05

Small rotation angles

If the angle of rotation φ\varphiφ is very small:

sin⁡φφ≈1.\frac{\sin\varphi}{\varphi}\approx1.φsinφ​≈1.

Neglecting second-degree terms:

R=[1−φzφyφz1−φx−φyφx1].\boldsymbol R=\begin{bmatrix}1&-\varphi_z&\varphi_y\\\varphi_z&1&-\varphi_x\\-\varphi_y&\varphi_x&1\end{bmatrix}.R=​1φz​−φy​​−φz​1φx​​φy​−φx​1​​.

If the rotation angle is small and second-order terms are retained, the rotation angles are assumed to be small, but not infinitesimal; therefore, the second-order terms cannot be neglected. Since the rotation matrix contains quadratic terms in the rotation components, the resulting problem is geometrically nonlinear.

sin⁡φφ≈1,1−cos⁡φφ2≈12.\frac{\sin\varphi}{\varphi}\approx1,\qquad\frac{1-\cos\varphi}{\varphi^2}\approx\frac12.φsinφ​≈1,φ21−cosφ​≈21​.

And:

R=[1−12(φy2+φz2)−φz+12φxφyφy+12φzφxφz+12φxφy1−12(φz2+φx2)−φx+12φyφz−φy+12φzφxφx+12φyφz1−12(φx2+φy2)].\boldsymbol R=\begin{bmatrix}1-\frac12(\varphi_y^2+\varphi_z^2)&-\varphi_z+\frac12\varphi_x\varphi_y&\varphi_y+\frac12\varphi_z\varphi_x\\\varphi_z+\frac12\varphi_x\varphi_y&1-\frac12(\varphi_z^2+\varphi_x^2)&-\varphi_x+\frac12\varphi_y\varphi_z\\-\varphi_y+\frac12\varphi_z\varphi_x&\varphi_x+\frac12\varphi_y\varphi_z&1-\frac12(\varphi_x^2+\varphi_y^2)\end{bmatrix}.R=​1−21​(φy2​+φz2​)φz​+21​φx​φy​−φy​+21​φz​φx​​−φz​+21​φx​φy​1−21​(φz2​+φx2​)φx​+21​φy​φz​​φy​+21​φz​φx​−φx​+21​φy​φz​1−21​(φx2​+φy2​)​​.

06

Example

The 3D part in the figure below must be divided into finite elements. The part can be divided into 12 identical slices. Only one slice will be divided into finite elements, and then, by rotating, reflecting and copying this slice, the entire FE mesh will be obtained.

One “slice” is depicted in the figure above, on the left. Nodal coordinates are known: x\boldsymbol xx is a matrix with nnn rows and 3 columns (for the three coordinates x,y,zx,y,zx,y,z); nnn is the number of nodes. Coordinates x\boldsymbol xx are in the global coordinate frame.

Firstly, this slice is rotated around the axis computed using the normalized cross product:

n1=n0×n∥n0×n∥,\boldsymbol n_1=\frac{\boldsymbol n_0\times\boldsymbol n}{\lVert\boldsymbol n_0\times\boldsymbol n\rVert},n1​=∥n0​×n∥n0​×n​,

with the angle computed using a dot product:

φ=arccos⁡(n0⋅n).\varphi=\arccos(\boldsymbol n_0\cdot\boldsymbol n).φ=arccos(n0​⋅n).

This gives the rotation matrix R1\boldsymbol R_1R1​:

R1=E+sin⁡φ n~1+(1−cos⁡φ)n~1 2.\boldsymbol R_1=\boldsymbol E+\sin\varphi\,\widetilde{\boldsymbol n}_1+(1-\cos\varphi)\widetilde{\boldsymbol n}_1^{\,2}.R1​=E+sinφn1​+(1−cosφ)n12​.

The new coordinates are:

xn=xR1T+Δx,\boldsymbol x_n=\boldsymbol x\boldsymbol R_1^T+\Delta\boldsymbol x,xn​=xR1T​+Δx,

where Δx\Delta\boldsymbol xΔx contains the translation components, applied to every row. For our example, the translation is [20105]\begin{bmatrix}20&10&5\end{bmatrix}[20​10​5​].

The remaining slices are obtained with the rotation matrix:

Rt=R2R1⟶xn=xRtT+Δx,\boldsymbol R_t=\boldsymbol R_2\boldsymbol R_1\quad\longrightarrow\quad\boldsymbol x_n=\boldsymbol x\boldsymbol R_t^T+\Delta\boldsymbol x,Rt​=R2​R1​⟶xn​=xRtT​+Δx,

where R2\boldsymbol R_2R2​ is a rotation around the axis defined by the unit vector n\boldsymbol nn (see the figure above):

R2=E+sin⁡φ0 n~+(1−cos⁡φ0)n~ 2.\boldsymbol R_2=\boldsymbol E+\sin\varphi_0\,\widetilde{\boldsymbol n}+(1-\cos\varphi_0)\widetilde{\boldsymbol n}^{\,2}.R2​=E+sinφ0​n+(1−cosφ0​)n2.

Angle φ0\varphi_0φ0​ is a multiple of π/3\pi/3π/3. The 12 slices are obtained at six angular positions spaced by π/3\pi/3π/3, with a reflected and a non-reflected slice at each position. For slices 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12, we need to apply a reflection to obtain the circular holes, with each slice having only a “half-hole”. So the transformation matrix becomes:

Rt=R2R1Ra⟶xn=xRtT+Δx,\boldsymbol R_t=\boldsymbol R_2\boldsymbol R_1\boldsymbol R_a\quad\longrightarrow\quad\boldsymbol x_n=\boldsymbol x\boldsymbol R_t^T+\Delta\boldsymbol x,Rt​=R2​R1​Ra​⟶xn​=xRtT​+Δx,

where:

Ra=[10001000−1].\boldsymbol R_a=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&-1\end{bmatrix}.Ra​=​100​010​00−1​​.

In fact, the matrix Ra\boldsymbol R_aRa​ allows us to build the symmetric slice with respect to the local xyxyxy-plane (reflection).

The chapter follows the direct and consistent Updated Lagrangian formulation developed in Section 15.5 and applied to HEX8 in Section 27.2. A simple isotropic nonlinear elastic model is defined by the relationship between equivalent strain and equivalent stress [1], [2], [3].

01

Nonlinear elastic model

Loading and unloading follow the same assumed constitutive curve, and no permanent strain remains after unloading. This distinguishes the model from plasticity, in which irreversible strain may remain. This describes the assumed loading–unloading relation, not a general energy-conservation property for arbitrary multiaxial loading histories.

The equivalent strain, also called von Mises strain [1], is:

04

Updated Lagrangian formulation

The direct and consistent UL formulation follows Section 15.5 and its HEX8 implementation in Section 27.2. The total Euler–Almansi strain tensor is obtained from:

e=12(I−F−TF−1)\boldsymbol{e}=\frac12\left(\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{F}^{-T}\boldsymbol{F}^{-1}\right)e=21​(I−F−TF−1)

Here, F\boldsymbol{F}F is the deformation gradient relating the initial and current configurations. For the current trial configuration, the element internal force vector is:

finte=∫ΩteBTσ dv\boldsymbol{f}_{int}^{e}=\int_{\Omega_t^e}\boldsymbol{B}^T\boldsymbol{\sigma}\,dvfinte​=∫Ωte​​BTσdv

Linearization of this expression gives the consistent element tangent stiffness matrix, with material and geometric contributions:

Kel=Kmat+Kgeom\boldsymbol{K}_{el}=\boldsymbol{K}_{mat}+\boldsymbol{K}_{geom}Kel​=Kmat​+Kgeom​

The material contribution is:

Kmat=∫ΩteBTDtBQ dv\boldsymbol{K}_{mat}=\int_{\Omega_t^e}\boldsymbol{B}^T\boldsymbol{D}_t\boldsymbol{B}_Q\,dvKmat​=∫Ωte​​BTDt​BQ​dv

The matrix BQ\boldsymbol{B}_QBQ​ accounts for the variation of the total Euler–Almansi strains with respect to the nodal displacements, as explained in Section 27.2. The expression for Kgeom\boldsymbol{K}_{geom}Kgeom​ remains the same as in that formulation, although its numerical value depends on the current stresses. The material contribution uses Dt\boldsymbol{D}_tDt​ instead of the initial elastic matrix D0\boldsymbol{D}_0D0​.

The nonlinear equilibrium equations are:

R(u)=fint(u)−fext=0\boldsymbol{R}(\boldsymbol{u})=\boldsymbol{f}_{int}(\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{f}_{ext}=\boldsymbol{0}R(u)=fint​(u)−fext​=0

At each Newton–Raphson iteration, the consistent tangent is used to calculate the displacement correction. Both the geometric and material nonlinearities are linearized.

05

MATLAB implementation

The subprogram stiff_cons.m evaluates total Euler–Almansi strain directly from the initial and current element configurations, computes stresses with the secant matrix, and uses the consistent material tangent in the spatial stiffness formulation.

The constitutive calculation distinguishes between the secant matrix used to calculate stresses,

σ=Dsε\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{D}_s\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}σ=Ds​ε

and the consistent material tangent used in the Newton–Raphson linearization:

dσ=Dt dεd\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{D}_t\,d\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}dσ=Dt​dε

The tangent must be obtained by differentiating the same interpolated constitutive relationship used for the stresses.

In gen.m, the supplied constitutive data are sampled by pchip at strain intervals of 0.001. Outside the initial linear branch, stiff_cons.m interpolates linearly between these samples. Its tangent uses the exact slope of the active linear segment, not the derivative of the continuous PCHIP curve. At an interpolation knot, the slope of the selected adjoining segment is used.

slope = (sgi(k+1)-sgi(k))/(eei(k+1)-eei(k));
sge = sgi(k) + slope*(eeq-eei(k));
alpha = sge/(E*eeq);
dalpha = (slope*eeq-sge)/(E*eeq^2);
Dtan = alpha*DHooke + sig0*(dalpha*deq);
sig = alpha*sig0;

Here, DHooke denotes D0\boldsymbol{D}_0D0​, sig0 = DHooke*ev', and deq is the gradient of equivalent strain. Only the six engineering strain components are stored:

strnt=zeros(8*nel,6,nstep+1);

The eight Gauss points follow the local HEX8 node order. The plotting routine averages the associated Gauss-point stresses at common nodes and uses the selected load step. The current Jacobian determinant is required to be positive.

The present formulation can be readily extended from the HEX8 to the HEX20 element, following the procedure described in Section 27.2 and Section 27.3. The nonlinear constitutive law and its consistent material tangent remain unchanged; only the interpolation functions, the dimensions of the element matrices, and the numerical integration scheme need to be adapted. Therefore, a separate MATLAB implementation for HEX20 is not provided here.

07

Newton–Raphson convergence

As equivalent strain increases, the constitutive curve becomes flatter. The tangent modulus decreases more rapidly than the secant modulus. Using the secant matrix alone in the material stiffness can therefore lead to progressively slower convergence. The consistent tangent includes the derivative of the nonlinear material law and improves convergence, particularly at high load levels.

The following controlled comparison uses the same 480-element mesh, loads, 30 load steps, tolerance and spatial residual. Only the material matrix in the tangent is changed; both runs use the same constitutive stress evaluation and geometric terms.

Material matrix in the stiffnessTotal Newton iterationsIterations at final step
Secant approximation38447
Consistent tangent15910

Both runs converge. At the adopted stopping tolerance, their final nodal displacements differ by at most 8.94×10−4 mm8.94\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{mm}8.94×10−4 mm. These figures describe this test, not a universal convergence rate.

The final equilibrium solution should not depend on the number of intermediate load steps, provided the same equilibrium branch is reached and convergence is sufficiently accurate. This is distinct from Newton–Raphson efficiency: load stepping may still be necessary for reliable convergence, and a single load step is not sufficient for every nonlinear problem.

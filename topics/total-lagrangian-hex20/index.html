01

Introduction

The Total Lagrangian formulation developed in Section 26.1 can be applied directly to the 20-node isoparametric hexahedral finite element. Therefore, only the modifications resulting from the higher-order interpolation are presented here. The geometry, node numbering, natural coordinates and interpolation functions of the HEX20 element were presented in Section 25.3.

The HEX20 element has 20 nodes and three displacement DOFs per node, therefore 60 element DOFs:

uel={u1v1w1⋯u20v20w20}T.\boldsymbol{u}_{el}=\begin{Bmatrix}u_1&v_1&w_1&\cdots&u_{20}&v_{20}&w_{20}\end{Bmatrix}^{T}.uel​={u1​​v1​​w1​​⋯​u20​​v20​​w20​​}T.

05

Results and comparison with ANSYS

The HEX20 MATLAB results agree very closely with the corresponding ANSYS model. Likewise, the HEX8 MATLAB results in Section 26.1 agree with the corresponding ANSYS HEX8 results.

The noticeable difference between the HEX8 and HEX20 diagrams is therefore not evidence of an error in either MATLAB implementation. Each implementation reproduces the corresponding ANSYS element behaviour.

As the HEX8 mesh is refined, its solution progressively approaches that obtained with the HEX20 element. The substantial differences observed for the coarse HEX8 mesh are therefore mainly associated with the lower-order approximation and mesh discretization.

The comparatively stiff response of the coarse HEX8 mesh is characteristic of shear locking, which may affect low-order fully integrated elements in bending-dominated problems. As the HEX8 mesh is refined, this artificial stiffness decreases and the solution progressively approaches that obtained with HEX20. The higher-order HEX20 element is considerably less sensitive to this effect.

Locking phenomena and possible remedies will be discussed separately in a future section.

07

Conclusions and stress plots

The convergence study shows that the HEX8 solution progressively approaches the HEX20 result as the mesh is refined. The HEX20 element therefore provides, for this bending-dominated problem, a comparable response with a substantially coarser mesh.

This conclusion is supported by the numerical convergence study and by the agreement of both MATLAB implementations with their corresponding ANSYS element models.

The remarks in Section 26.1 concerning stress measures and the representation of stresses on the deformed configuration apply unchanged to HEX20. The transformation between second Piola–Kirchhoff and Cauchy stresses is discussed in Chapter 14.

In the Total Lagrangian formulation, the initial undeformed configuration is the reference configuration. All derivatives, Jacobians and volume integrals are evaluated with respect to that initial configuration. The development follows Section 12.2; the HEX8 interpolation and linear formulation are presented in Section 25.1.

01

Strain and stress notation

The Green–Lagrange strain vector and the corresponding stress vector are:

ε={εxεyεzγxyγyzγzx},σ={σxσyσzτxyτyzτzx}.\boldsymbol\varepsilon=\begin{Bmatrix}\varepsilon_x\\\varepsilon_y\\\varepsilon_z\\\gamma_{xy}\\\gamma_{yz}\\\gamma_{zx}\end{Bmatrix},\qquad\boldsymbol\sigma=\begin{Bmatrix}\sigma_x\\\sigma_y\\\sigma_z\\\tau_{xy}\\\tau_{yz}\\\tau_{zx}\end{Bmatrix}.ε=⎩⎨⎧​εx​εy​εz​γxy​γyz​γzx​​⎭⎬⎫​,σ=⎩⎨⎧​σx​σy​σz​τxy​τyz​τzx​​⎭⎬⎫​.

Remark. In this section the symbol ε\boldsymbol\varepsilonε is retained for the Green–Lagrange strain vector, although this strain measure is different from the infinitesimal strain used in linear analysis. Similarly, the symbol σ\boldsymbol\sigmaσ is retained for convenience for the stress measure energetically conjugate to the Green–Lagrange strain, i.e. the second Piola–Kirchhoff stress. Since small strains and a linear elastic material are assumed, the familiar linear-elastic constitutive relation is used:

σ=Dε.\boldsymbol\sigma=\boldsymbol D\boldsymbol\varepsilon.σ=Dε.

This notation simplifies the transition from the linear formulation while preserving the Total Lagrangian character of the analysis.

08

Tangent stiffness matrix

The first variation of the residual is:

δΨ=Ael=1nel[∑GδBTσdet⁡(J0)+∑GBTδσdet⁡(J0)]=Ktδu.\delta\boldsymbol\Psi=\mathcal A_{el=1}^{n_{el}}\left[\sum_G\delta\boldsymbol B^T\boldsymbol\sigma\det(\boldsymbol J_0)+\sum_G\boldsymbol B^T\delta\boldsymbol\sigma\det(\boldsymbol J_0)\right]=\boldsymbol K_t\delta\boldsymbol u.δΨ=Ael=1nel​​[G∑​δBTσdet(J0​)+G∑​BTδσdet(J0​)]=Kt​δu.

Writing the two parts of the strain–displacement matrix explicitly:

δΨ=Ael=1nel[∑Gδ(B0T+BLT)σdet⁡(J0)+∑G(B0T+BLT)δσdet⁡(J0)]=Ktδu.\delta\boldsymbol\Psi=\mathcal A_{el=1}^{n_{el}}\left[\sum_G\delta\left(\boldsymbol B_0^T+\boldsymbol B_L^T\right)\boldsymbol\sigma\det(\boldsymbol J_0)+\sum_G\left(\boldsymbol B_0^T+\boldsymbol B_L^T\right)\delta\boldsymbol\sigma\det(\boldsymbol J_0)\right]=\boldsymbol K_t\delta\boldsymbol u.δΨ=Ael=1nel​​[G∑​δ(B0T​+BLT​)σdet(J0​)+G∑​(B0T​+BLT​)δσdet(J0​)]=Kt​δu.

δB=δ(B0+BL)=δBL.\delta\boldsymbol B=\delta(\boldsymbol B_0+\boldsymbol B_L)=\delta\boldsymbol B_L.δB=δ(B0​+BL​)=δBL​.

09

Newton–Raphson solution and convergence

At iteration i, the Newton–Raphson equations are:

Δu(i)=−(Kt(i))−1Ψ(i),u(i+1)=u(i)+Δu(i),\Delta\boldsymbol u^{(i)}=-\left(\boldsymbol K_t^{(i)}\right)^{-1}\boldsymbol\Psi^{(i)},\qquad\boldsymbol u^{(i+1)}=\boldsymbol u^{(i)}+\Delta\boldsymbol u^{(i)},Δu(i)=−(Kt(i)​)−1Ψ(i),u(i+1)=u(i)+Δu(i),
Ψ(i)=Ψ(u(i)),kel=∂fel∂uel.\boldsymbol\Psi^{(i)}=\boldsymbol\Psi(\boldsymbol u^{(i)}),\qquad\boldsymbol k_{el}=\frac{\partial\boldsymbol f_{el}}{\partial\boldsymbol u_{el}}.Ψ(i)=Ψ(u(i)),kel​=∂uel​∂fel​​.

The element force vectors and tangent stiffness matrices are assembled to obtain Ψ(i)\boldsymbol\Psi^{(i)}Ψ(i) and Kt(i)\boldsymbol K_t^{(i)}Kt(i)​. The iterative process is stopped when the error is smaller than the prescribed tolerance. An absolute displacement-increment measure is:

ηa=ΔuTΔun.\eta_a=\sqrt{\frac{\Delta\boldsymbol u^T\Delta\boldsymbol u}{n}}.ηa​=nΔuTΔu​​.

In the supplied program, the normalization uses neq, the number of equations.

A relative displacement criterion may also be used, for example:

ηu=∥Δu∥max⁡(∥u∥,uref),\eta_u=\frac{\|\Delta\boldsymbol u\|}{\max(\|\boldsymbol u\|,u_{ref})},ηu​=max(∥u∥,uref​)∥Δu∥​,

urefu_{ref}uref​ being a small reference displacement preventing division by a very small value at the beginning of the iterations. A residual-based convergence criterion may also be used together with the displacement criterion.

Important remark. In the Total Lagrangian formulation the total strain is evaluated directly with respect to the initial configuration; it is not obtained by accumulating strain increments. Therefore, provided that the same equilibrium solution is reached, the accuracy of the final result does not depend on the number of load steps. Load stepping is nevertheless useful, and sometimes necessary, to improve Newton–Raphson convergence and to follow the equilibrium path.

12

Stress plots

Remark on stress plots. Although the stress components are calculated with respect to the global coordinate system, their representation on the deformed configuration is often more intuitive. For example, the σx\sigma_xσx​ stress map then follows the deformed beam and can be interpreted approximately as the stress tangent to its fibres.

The stresses obtained directly from the Total Lagrangian constitutive relation are referred to the initial configuration. If stresses in the current configuration are required, they can be transformed to Cauchy stresses according to:

σC=1JFSFT,\boldsymbol\sigma_C=\frac1J\boldsymbol F\boldsymbol S\boldsymbol F^T,σC​=J1​FSFT,

where S\boldsymbol SS is the second Piola–Kirchhoff stress tensor represented in this section by the vector σ\boldsymbol\sigmaσ. In this transformation, F\boldsymbol FF denotes the deformation gradient and J=det⁡(F)J=\det(\boldsymbol F)J=det(F). The stress measures and this transformation are discussed in Chapter 14.

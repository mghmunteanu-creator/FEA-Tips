Introduction

In Section 27.2, a direct Updated Lagrangian formulation with a consistent tangent stiffness matrix was developed for the eight-node isoparametric HEX8 element. Total Euler–Almansi strains were evaluated directly from the initial and current configurations, and the tangent stiffness matrix was obtained by consistently linearizing the internal nodal-force vector.

The same formulation is extended here to the twenty-node isoparametric HEX20 element. This development follows the approach adopted in Section 15.6, where the consistent formulation for the Q4 element was extended to Q8.

The theoretical principles remain unchanged. Consequently, only the modifications required by the quadratic interpolation, the numerical integration procedure, and the corresponding numerical results are presented below.

The spatial kinematics and linearization underlying this formulation are discussed by Bonet and Wood [1].

02

HEX20 element formulation

The HEX20 element has twenty nodes, each with three displacement degrees of freedom, giving a total of sixty element degrees of freedom.

The main matrix dimensions are:

MatrixDimensions
Natural-coordinate derivative matrix H\boldsymbol{H}H3 × 20
Strain–displacement matrix B\boldsymbol{B}B6 × 60
Modified strain–displacement matrix BQ\boldsymbol{B}_{Q}BQ​6 × 60
Constitutive matrix D\boldsymbol{D}D6 × 6
Element tangent stiffness matrix kel\boldsymbol{k}_{el}kel​60 × 60

The total deformation gradient, Euler–Almansi strains, Cauchy stresses, internal nodal-force vector, and consistent tangent stiffness matrix are calculated using the expressions derived in Section 27.2.

The twenty quadratic interpolation functions and their natural-coordinate derivatives are evaluated by the MATLAB function shape20.m. These derivatives are used to construct the matrix H\boldsymbol{H}H at each integration point.

The principal additional change concerns numerical integration. Whereas the HEX8 element uses a 2 × 2 × 2 Gauss rule, the HEX20 element employs a 3 × 3 × 3 rule, with twenty-seven integration points per element.

The one-dimensional Gauss coordinates and weights are:

The three-dimensional rule is obtained from all twenty-seven combinations of these coordinates. Each integration weight is the product of the corresponding three one-dimensional weights.

At every Gauss point, the internal nodal-force vector and consistent tangent stiffness matrix are evaluated and accumulated over the element volume.

The expressions for the material and geometric tangent contributions remain those developed in Section 27.2, with the corresponding HEX20 matrix dimensions.

As in the HEX8 formulation, the current geometry is updated at each Newton iteration, while the total Euler–Almansi strains are evaluated directly from the initial and current configurations.

For HEX20, the minimum vertical displacement agrees very closely with ANSYS, with a relative difference of approximately 0.23%. The difference in the maximum vertical displacement is larger, approximately 4.31%, and is retained explicitly in the comparison. Both relative differences use the magnitude of the corresponding ANSYS value as the denominator.

These results illustrate the use of the quadratic HEX20 element in three-dimensional large-displacement analysis and the practical independence of the converged displacement solution from load-step subdivision in the tested cases.

04

MATLAB programs

The accompanying MATLAB programs implement the direct Updated Lagrangian formulation with a consistent tangent stiffness matrix for twenty-node isoparametric HEX20 elements.

The program main.m controls the load steps and Newton iterations, while gen.m generates the HEX20 mesh, boundary conditions and nodal loads.

The function shape20.m evaluates the quadratic interpolation functions and their natural-coordinate derivatives.

The script stiff_cons20.m evaluates the total Euler–Almansi strains, Cauchy stresses, internal nodal forces and consistent tangent stiffness matrix using twenty-seven Gauss points per element.

Compared with the corresponding HEX8 routine in Section 27.2, stiff_cons20.m retains essentially the same formulation. The modifications mainly concern the quadratic interpolation, the dimensions of the element matrices, and the use of a 3 × 3 × 3 Gauss integration rule.

The remaining routines provide the mesh and results visualization.

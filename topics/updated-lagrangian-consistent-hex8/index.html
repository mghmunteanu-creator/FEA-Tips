Introduction

StrainSmall
DisplacementLarge
MaterialLinear elastic
Stress stateThree-dimensional

This section extends Section 15.5 from the two-dimensional Q4 element to the three-dimensional HEX8 element, retaining its direct evaluation of total Euler–Almansi strains and consistent linearization of the internal-force vector. In Section 27.1, total Euler–Almansi strains are combined with an incremental Green–Lagrange stiffness formulation; here the tangent is the consistent derivative of the adopted discrete internal forces.

The nodal coordinates are updated after each displacement correction, and the subsequent calculations are performed with reference to the current trial configuration. The approximate formulation of Section 27.1 provides accurate results even for relatively few load steps; its results approach the consistent solution as the number of load steps increases.

In this section, a direct formulation with a consistent tangent stiffness matrix is developed for the eight-node isoparametric HEX8 element. Total Euler–Almansi strains are calculated directly from the initial and current configurations, without accumulating strain increments. The tangent stiffness matrix is obtained by consistently linearizing the internal-force vector and accounting for every factor that depends on the nodal displacements.

The converged results for the numerical example are practically independent of the number of load steps, within the prescribed tolerance. The solutions obtained with the approximate formulations of Section 27.1 approach these results as the load-step size decreases.

The derivation follows the spatial description of nonlinear equilibrium and its linearization discussed by Bonet and Wood [1], here specialized to the HEX8 element and to the adopted three-dimensional constitutive model.

δu(x,y,z)=∑j=18Njδuj,δuj=[δuj δvj δwj]T\delta\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{u}}(x,y,z)=\sum_{j=1}^{8}N_j\delta\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{u}}_j,\quad\delta\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{u}}_j=[\delta u_j\ \delta v_j\ \delta w_j]^Tδu(x,y,z)=j=1∑8​Nj​δuj​,δuj​=[δuj​ δvj​ δwj​]T

δL=∑j=18δujbjT=[δu1 ⋯ δu8]bT\delta\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{L}}=\sum_{j=1}^{8}\delta\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{u}}_j\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}_j^T=[\delta\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{u}}_1\ \cdots\ \delta\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{u}}_8]\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}^TδL=j=1∑8​δuj​bjT​=[δu1​ ⋯ δu8​]bT

Substituting in the strain variation:

δe=12∑j=18[QδujbjT+bjδujTQ]\delta \htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{e}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum _{j=1}^{8} \left[ \htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{Q}}\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{j}{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}}_{j}^{T}+{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}}_{j}\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{j}^{T}\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{Q}}\right] δe=21​j=1∑8​[Qδuj​bjT​+bj​δujT​Q]

In Voigt notation, recalling that the three shear components are engineering shear strains, the previous relation gives

δεv=BQδuel\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon }}}_{v}={\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}}_{Q}\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{el}δεv​=BQ​δuel​

where:

δuel=[δu1 δv1 δw1 ⋯ δu8 δv8 δw8]T\delta\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{u}}_{el}=[\delta u_1\ \delta v_1\ \delta w_1\ \cdots\ \delta u_8\ \delta v_8\ \delta w_8]^Tδuel​=[δu1​ δv1​ δw1​ ⋯ δu8​ δv8​ δw8​]T

The matrix BQ{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}}_{Q}BQ​ has the 6×36\times 36×3 nodal blocks

BQ=[B1Q ⋯ B8Q]∈R6×24\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}_Q=[\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}_1\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{Q}}\ \cdots\ \htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}_8\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{Q}}]\in\mathbb{R}^{6\times24}BQ​=[B1​Q ⋯ B8​Q]∈R6×24

Thus the blocks of the standard strain–displacement matrix are multiplied by Q\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{Q}}Q. This operation is performed directly at each Gauss point in MATLAB:

BQ=zeros(6,24);

    ii=3*i-2:3*i;
    BQ(:,ii)=B(:,ii)*Q;
end

For Q=I3\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{Q}}={\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{I}}}_{3}Q=I3​, the standard matrix is recovered: BQ=B{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}}_{Q}=\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}BQ​=B.

06

Consistent linearization of the internal forces

The element tangent stiffness matrix is defined as

kel=∂fel∂uel{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{k}}}_{el}=\frac{\partial {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{f}}}_{el}}{\partial {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{el}}kel​=∂uel​∂fel​​

The variation is infinitesimal and is evaluated about the current configuration. Using 2×2×22\times 2\times 22×2×2 Gauss integration, the element internal-force vector is approximated by

fel=∑p=18BpTσv,pJv,p{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{f}}}_{el}=\sum _{p=1}^{8} {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}}_{p}^{T}{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\sigma }}}_{v,p}J_{v,p}fel​=p=1∑8​BpT​σv,p​Jv,p​

All integration weights are equal to one for this rule. Three factors depend on the current nodal displacements: Bp\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}_pBp​, σv,p\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{v,p}σv,p​ and the volume Jacobian Jv,p=∣detJp∣J_{v,p}=|\mathit{det}\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{J}}_p|Jv,p​=∣detJp​∣. For a nonsingular element with unchanged orientation, its variation satisfies the same determinant identity used below.

The first variation of the internal-force vector is therefore

δfel=∑p=18[BpTDδεv,p Jv,p+δBpTσv,p Jv,p+BpTσv,p δJv,p]\begin{aligned}\delta\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{f}}_{el}=\sum_{p=1}^{8}\big[&\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}_p^T\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{D}}\delta\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}_{v,p}\,J_{v,p}\\&+\delta\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}_p^T\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{v,p}\,J_{v,p}\\&+\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}_p^T\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{v,p}\,\delta J_{v,p}\big]\end{aligned}δfel​=p=1∑8​[​BpT​Dδεv,p​Jv,p​+δBpT​σv,p​Jv,p​+BpT​σv,p​δJv,p​]​

Each contribution will now be derived separately.

6.1. Variation of the Euler–Almansi strains: material tangent

From the preceding derivation,

δεv=BQδuel\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon }}}_{v}={\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}}_{Q}\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{el}δεv​=BQ​δuel​

As D\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{D}}D is constant for the adopted constitutive model,

δσv=DBQδuel\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\sigma }}}_{v}=\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{D}}{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}}_{Q}\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{el}δσv​=DBQ​δuel​

The first contribution to the tangent stiffness matrix is therefore

kelm=∑p=18BpTDBQ,pJv,p{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{k}}}_{e{l}_{m}}=\sum _{p=1}^{8} {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}}_{p}^{T}\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{D}}{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}}_{Q,p}J_{v,p}kelm​​=p=1∑8​BpT​DBQ,p​Jv,p​

This is the material contribution. The two strain–displacement matrices play different roles: BpT{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}}_{p}^{T}BpT​ comes from the spatial internal-force expression, whereas BQ,p{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}}_{Q,p}BQ,p​ describes the variation of the total Euler–Almansi strains.

6.2. Variation of the strain–displacement matrix

The matrix B\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}B depends on the spatial gradients of the interpolation functions. These gradients change with the current element geometry.

For node iii,

BiTσv=[σxτxyτzxτxyσyτyzτzxτyzσz]bi=σbi\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}_i^T\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_v=\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_x&\tau_{xy}&\tau_{zx}\\\tau_{xy}&\sigma_y&\tau_{yz}\\\tau_{zx}&\tau_{yz}&\sigma_z\end{bmatrix}\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}_i=\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}_iBiT​σv​=​σx​τxy​τzx​​τxy​σy​τyz​​τzx​τyz​σz​​​bi​=σbi​

Since bi=J−1Hi{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}}_{i}={\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{J}}}^{-1}{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{H}}}_{i}bi​=J−1Hi​, where Hi{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{H}}}_{i}Hi​ is the iiith column of H\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{H}}H and is independent of the nodal displacements,

The variation of the current Jacobian is:

δJ=JδLT\delta \htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{J}}=\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{J}}\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{L}}}^{T}δJ=JδLT

Consequently:

δbi=−J−1(δJ)bi=−δLTbi\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}}_{i}=-{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{J}}}^{-1}\left( \delta \htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{J}}\right) {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}}_{i}=-\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{L}}}^{T}{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}}_{i}δbi​=−J−1(δJ)bi​=−δLTbi​

The contribution to the variation of the internal nodal force associated with node iii at one Gauss point is:

δfi(B)=Jv σδbi\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{f}}}_{i}^{\left( B\right) }=J_v\, \htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\sigma }}\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}}_{i}δfi(B)​=Jv​σδbi​

6.3. Variation of the Jacobian determinant

For any nonsingular matrix, the determinant variation identity is:

δ(Jv)=Jv tr(J−1δJ)\delta \left( J_v\right) =J_v\, \mathit{tr}\left( {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{J}}}^{-1}\delta \htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{J}}\right) δ(Jv​)=Jv​tr(J−1δJ)

Using δJ=JδLT\delta \htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{J}}=\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{J}}\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{L}}}^{T}δJ=JδLT gives

J−1δJ=δLT{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{J}}}^{-1}\delta \htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{J}}=\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{L}}}^{T}J−1δJ=δLT

and hence:

δ(Jv)=Jv tr(δL)\delta \left( J_v\right) =J_v\, \mathit{tr}\left( \delta \htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{L}}\right) δ(Jv​)=Jv​tr(δL)

For node iii, the corresponding internal-force variation is:

δfi(J)=σbi δ(Jv)\delta {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{f}}}_{i}^{\left( J\right) }=\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\sigma }}{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}}_{i}\, \delta \left( J_v\right) δfi(J)​=σbi​δ(Jv​)

6.4. Geometric contribution to the tangent stiffness matrix

Combining the variations of B\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{B}}B and JvJ_vJv​, we obtain the geometric contribution to the tangent stiffness matrix. The 3×33\times 33×3 block associated with nodes iii and jjj is:

kelg,ij=∑p=18Jv,p[(σpbi,p)bj,pT−(σpbj,p)bi,pT]{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{k}}}_{e{l}_{g,ij}}=\sum _{p=1}^{8} J_{v,p}\left[ \left( {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\sigma }}}_{p}{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}}_{i,p}\right) {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}}_{j,p}^{T}-\left( {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{\sigma }}}_{p}{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}}_{j,p}\right) {\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{b}}}_{i,p}^{T}\right] kelg,ij​​=p=1∑8​Jv,p​[(σp​bi,p​)bj,pT​−(σp​bj,p​)bi,pT​]

Here ppp indexes the Gauss points, and i,ji,ji,j index the nodes. Each block is 3×33\times 33×3; arranging the sixty-four blocks yields the 24×2424\times 2424×24 matrix kelg{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{k}}}_{e{l}_{g}}kelg​​. The index ggg denotes geometric; the index ppp denotes the Gauss point.

The corresponding MATLAB implementation evaluates the geometric contribution at one Gauss point, before multiplying by the current Jacobian determinant:

kg=zeros(24);
for i=1:8;
    ii=3*i-2:3*i;
    bi=b(:,i);
    sbi=sigma*bi;
    for j=1:8;
        jj=3*j-2:3*j;
        bj=b(:,j);
        kg(ii,jj)=sbi*bj'-(sigma*bj)*bi';
    end
end

The geometric contribution is obtained by direct linearization of the adopted spatial internal-force vector. The resulting tangent matrix is not necessarily symmetric; it should not be artificially symmetrized.

6.5. Complete tangent stiffness matrix

The complete element tangent stiffness matrix is:

kel=kelm+kelg\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{k}}_{el}=\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{k}}_{el_m}+\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{k}}_{el_g}kel​=kelm​​+kelg​​

The material part describes the variation of the Cauchy stresses through the total Euler–Almansi strains. The geometric part describes the variations of the spatial interpolation derivatives and the volume Jacobian. Integration is directly over the three-dimensional volume; there is no separate thickness factor or thickness contribution.

For one Gauss point, the MATLAB assembly statement is

kel=kel+(km+kg)*detJ;

The assembled matrix is the consistent derivative of the adopted discrete internal-force vector, including its approximate constitutive law.

MATLAB programs

The accompanying MATLAB programs implement the Updated Lagrangian formulation for eight-node isoparametric HEX8 elements with 2×2×22\times 2\times 22×2×2 Gauss integration. The main program performs the load-step and Newton-iteration loops and updates the nodal coordinates after each displacement correction.

The script stiff_cons.m calculates the element internal forces and assembles the global tangent stiffness matrix. At each Gauss point, the initial and current Jacobian matrices are used to compute the total deformation gradient, the Euler–Almansi strains and the Cauchy stresses. The material and geometric terms are then combined.

The files plot_disp.m and plot_stress.m display the nodal displacements and the computed stresses.

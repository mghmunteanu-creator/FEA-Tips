Introduction

In Section 15.1, the Updated Lagrangian (UL) formulation was developed using a simplified strain–displacement matrix. In particular, the variation of the element geometry through the Jacobian determinant was neglected in the tangent stiffness matrix, and the variation of the Euler–Almansi strains was treated approximately.

Sections 15.2–15.4 introduced alternative incremental formulations combining Euler–Almansi and Green–Lagrange strain measures. The nodal coordinates are updated after each load step, and the subsequent calculations are performed with reference to the updated configuration. These approaches provide accurate results even for relatively few load steps; their accuracy generally improves as the number of load steps increases.

In this section, a direct formulation with a consistent tangent stiffness matrix is developed for the four-node isoparametric Q4 element. Total Euler–Almansi strains are calculated directly from the initial and current configurations, without accumulating strain increments. The tangent stiffness matrix is obtained by consistently linearizing the internal-force vector and accounting for every factor that depends on the nodal displacements.

The converged results for the numerical example are practically independent of the number of load steps, within the prescribed tolerance. The solutions obtained with the approximate formulations of the preceding sections approach these results as the load-step size decreases.

The derivation follows the spatial description of nonlinear equilibrium and its linearization discussed by Bonet and Wood [1], here specialized to the Q4 element and to the adopted plane-stress constitutive model.

The corresponding MATLAB implementation evaluates the geometric contribution at one Gauss point, before multiplying by the current thickness and Jacobian determinant:

kg=zeros(8);
for i=1:4
    ii=2*i-1:2*i;
    bi=b(:,i);
    sbi=sigma*bi;
    for j=1:4
        jj=2*j-1:2*j;
        bj=b(:,j);
        kg(ii,jj)=sbi*bj'-(sigma*bj)*bi';
    end
end

The geometric contribution is obtained by direct linearization of the adopted spatial internal-force vector. The resulting tangent matrix is not necessarily symmetric; it should not be artificially symmetrized.

The element thickness changes with deformation. For the adopted approximate plane-stress thickness update,

6.6. Complete tangent stiffness matrix

The complete element tangent stiffness matrix is:

kel=kelm+kelg+kelh.{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{k}}}}_{el}={\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{k}}}}_{e{l}_{m}}+{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{k}}}}_{e{l}_{g}}+{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\htmlClass{fea-math-bold-italic}{\boldsymbol{k}}}}_{e{l}_{h}}.kel​=kelm​​+kelg​​+kelh​​.

The material part describes the variation of the Cauchy stresses through the total Euler–Almansi strains. The geometric part describes the variations of the spatial interpolation derivatives and the Jacobian determinant. The thickness part describes the dependence of h1{h}_{1}h1​ on the current displacements.

For one Gauss point, the MATLAB assembly statement is

kel=kel+detJ*(th1*(km+kg)+(B'*sig)*dth);

The assembled matrix is the consistent derivative of the adopted discrete internal-force vector, including its approximate constitutive and thickness laws.

MATLAB programs

The accompanying MATLAB programs implement the Updated Lagrangian formulation for four-node isoparametric Q4 elements with 2×22\times 22×2 Gauss integration. The main program performs the load-step and Newton-iteration loops and updates the nodal coordinates after each displacement correction.

The script stiff_cons.m calculates the element internal forces and assembles the global tangent stiffness matrix. At each Gauss point, the initial and current Jacobian matrices are used to compute the total deformation gradient, the Euler–Almansi strains and the Cauchy stresses. The material, geometric and thickness terms are then combined.

The files plot_disp.m and plot_stress.m display the nodal displacements and the computed stresses.
